# 30 Difficult Legal-Bot Training Questions

**Jurisdiction:** England and Wales, using the law in force on the date the answer is generated.

## Question 1 — 1,000 words — Problem — Contract Law

A software company agrees to build a bespoke AI recruitment platform for £180,000, promising delivery by 1 June. The contract states that “time is of the essence” and limits the supplier’s liability to £25,000.

The platform is delivered on 20 June. Several important functions do not work, and the customer claims that the delay caused it to lose a commercial contract worth £300,000. The supplier argues that the customer would probably have lost the contract anyway and that the exclusion clause limits any recovery.

Advise the customer on breach, classification of contractual terms, termination, causation, remoteness, mitigation, damages and the enforceability of the limitation clause.

## Question 2 — 1,000 words — Problem — Consumer Law

Maya buys a £4,500 laptop from TechHub Ltd after a salesperson tells her that it is “specifically designed for professional 8K video editing” and will remain suitable for at least five years.

Within four months, the laptop repeatedly overheats when Maya edits 8K footage. TechHub relies on written terms stating that sales staff cannot make binding representations and that the customer’s only remedy is repair. The manufacturer offers to replace an internal component but refuses a refund.

Advise Maya on satisfactory quality, fitness for purpose, description, pre-contract statements, statutory remedies, repair, replacement, rejection, misrepresentation and attempts to restrict consumer rights.

## Question 3 — 1,000 words — Essay — Tort Law

“The modern law governing duties of care is not based on one coherent legal test, but on a collection of policy-driven exceptions.”

Critically assess this statement.

Your answer should consider incremental development, assumption of responsibility, omissions, public-authority liability, pure economic loss and the role of foreseeability, proximity, fairness and precedent.

## Question 4 — 1,000 words — Problem — Criminal Law

During a protest, Aaron deliberately pushes a security officer in order to move him away from a doorway. The officer falls, strikes his head and suffers a serious injury. Because of an undiagnosed medical condition, the injury becomes fatal.

Later that night, Ben believes that the officer is still alive and strikes him with a metal bar, intending to kill him. Medical evidence shows that the officer had probably died shortly before Ben struck him, although the time of death cannot be established conclusively.

Advise Aaron and Ben on homicide, unlawful act manslaughter, causation, mens rea, attempts and any relevant defences.

## Question 5 — 1,000 words — Problem — Professional Negligence

A solicitor uses an AI limitation-calculation tool when advising a client about a negligence claim. The tool produces the wrong date, and the solicitor fails to verify it manually. Proceedings are commenced after the applicable limitation period has expired.

The solicitor argues that the client failed to provide complete medical records and that the underlying negligence claim had only a 40% chance of success.

Advise the client on duty, breach, scope of duty, causation, loss of a chance, contributory negligence, recoverable loss and the relevance of the solicitor’s reliance on AI.

## Question 6 — 2,000 words — Essay — Criminal Evidence

“The English courts’ power to exclude improperly obtained evidence gives defendants weaker protection than a strict exclusionary rule would provide.”

Critically assess this statement.

Consider confessions, oppression, unreliability, improperly obtained evidence, fairness, identification evidence, hearsay and judicial discretion. Address the competing objectives of reliable fact-finding, protection of defendants and public confidence in criminal justice.

## Question 7 — 2,000 words — Problem — Employment and Equality Law

A technology company introduces an AI system that scores employees for promotion and redundancy. The system gives lower scores to:

- older workers;
- women returning from maternity leave;
- disabled employees with periods of sickness absence; and
- employees who previously complained about discrimination.

Several employees are dismissed after managers rely on the scores without conducting independent assessments. The employer argues that the system was designed by an external supplier and was intended to improve efficiency.

Advise the affected employees and the employer on unfair dismissal, redundancy, direct and indirect discrimination, discrimination arising from disability, reasonable adjustments, victimisation, justification, evidential burdens and remedies.

## Question 8 — 2,000 words — Problem — Land Law

A owns registered freehold land. A orally promises B that B may live in the property for life if B pays for major renovations. B spends £120,000 improving the property and moves into it.

A subsequently grants C a seven-year lease, but the lease is not registered. A then sells the property to D. D knows that B occupies the property but makes no detailed enquiries. D argues that neither B nor C has a registered interest.

Advise B, C and D on proprietary estoppel, constructive trusts where relevant, leases, overriding interests, actual occupation, priorities and registration.

## Question 9 — 2,000 words — Essay — Public Law

“Judicial review has developed from a mechanism for controlling the legal limits of government power into a constitutional system for protecting substantive rights.”

Critically evaluate this proposition.

Your answer should consider illegality, procedural fairness, legitimate expectation, irrationality, proportionality, justiciability, ouster clauses and the constitutional relationship between Parliament, the executive and the courts.

## Question 10 — 2,000 words — Problem — Company Law

The directors of Alpha Ltd learn that a major customer wishes to award Alpha a valuable supply contract. One director secretly forms a separate company and causes the customer to award the contract to that company instead.

The same director also receives an undisclosed payment from a supplier. After discovering the conduct, the controlling shareholders pass a resolution purporting to approve everything the director has done. A minority shareholder wishes to bring proceedings.

Advise the parties on directors’ duties, corporate opportunities, conflicts of interest, secret profits, ratification, derivative claims, unfair prejudice and available remedies.

## Question 11 — 3,000 words — Problem — Family Law

Daniel and Priya have been married for fourteen years and have two children aged six and eleven. Priya gave up a highly paid career after the first child was born. Daniel now earns £420,000 per year and owns shares in a technology company that he founded three years before the marriage.

The family home is worth £2.8 million and was partly purchased using inherited money. Shortly before separation, Daniel transfers £700,000 to his brother, claiming that it represents repayment of an old loan.

Priya seeks to remain in the family home and asks for substantial financial provision. Daniel argues that the company, inheritance and premarital assets should be excluded.

Advise both parties on needs, sharing, compensation, matrimonial and non-matrimonial property, inherited wealth, business assets, pensions, children and transactions intended to defeat financial claims.

## Question 12 — 3,000 words — Essay — Human Rights and Constitutional Law

“The Human Rights Act preserves parliamentary sovereignty in formal legal theory while substantially redistributing constitutional power towards the judiciary in practice.”

Critically evaluate this statement.

Consider statutory interpretation, declarations of incompatibility, public-authority liability, proportionality, positive obligations, horizontal effect, parliamentary responses and the relationship between domestic courts and the European Court of Human Rights.

## Question 13 — 3,000 words — Problem — Equity and Trusts

T’s will leaves £1 million to R, stating that T is “confident that R will use the money for my most loyal employees.”

T separately transfers shares to S, saying: “Hold these for my children until they are mature enough.” T also tells U that U is to receive a valuable painting, but title to the painting is never formally transferred.

After T’s death:

- R uses half of the £1 million for herself;
- S invests the shares in a business in which S has a personal interest;
- one beneficiary assigns their equitable interest to X; and
- some misapplied trust money is paid into a mixed bank account.

Analyse certainty, constitution, imperfect gifts, trusts arising by will, fiduciary duties, breach of trust, tracing, proprietary remedies, personal remedies and third-party liability.

## Question 14 — 3,000 words — Essay — Civil Litigation

“The overriding objective has transformed civil litigation from a system primarily concerned with determining substantive rights into one increasingly concerned with procedural discipline, efficiency and proportionality.”

Critically analyse this claim.

Your answer should consider case management, sanctions, relief from sanctions, disclosure, witness and expert evidence, summary judgment, strike-out, ADR, costs, Part 36 and the tension between finality, efficiency and access to justice.

## Question 15 — 3,000 words — Problem — Intellectual Property

Nova Games Ltd employs Aisha as a software developer. In her spare time, Aisha creates an AI-assisted gaming engine using sections of code that she originally developed while working on a Nova project.

She trains an image-generation module using thousands of online artworks without obtaining express licences. Nova later incorporates parts of the engine into a commercial game.

A competing developer claims that sections of the engine are substantially similar to its proprietary software. An artist discovers that generated game imagery closely resembles one of her illustrations. Aisha claims ownership of the engine and demands royalties.

Advise the parties on ownership, employee-created works, originality, substantial copying, computer programs, training material, generated outputs, confidential information, licensing and remedies.

## Question 16 — 4,000 words — Problem — Wills and Succession

George dies leaving an estate worth £9 million. His 2018 will leaves most of the estate to his three children.

In 2025, while seriously ill, George signs a new document leaving 80% of the estate to his live-in carer, Nina. One witness signs in George’s presence. The second witness signs later after Nina tells the witness that George has already executed the document.

Medical records show fluctuating confusion caused by medication. Nina arranged the solicitor’s appointment and was present during several discussions. After George’s death, an unsigned handwritten note is found stating that the family home should pass to his eldest daughter.

Advise the potential beneficiaries on execution formalities, testamentary capacity, knowledge and approval, undue influence, revocation, interpretation, intestacy and claims for reasonable financial provision.

## Question 17 — 4,000 words — Essay — Corporate Governance

“Directors’ duties are formally owed to the company, but modern company law increasingly expects directors to balance the interests of shareholders, creditors, employees, consumers and wider society.”

Critically assess this statement.

Discuss proper purposes, promotion of the success of the company, independent judgment, care and skill, conflicts of interest, stakeholder considerations, creditor interests during financial distress, ratification, derivative claims and the practical limits of enforcement.

## Question 18 — 4,000 words — Problem — Banking, Fraud and Restitution

A finance director at Orion Ltd receives an email apparently sent by the chief executive instructing her to transfer £4 million urgently to an acquisition adviser. The fraudsters use a compromised email account and an AI-generated voice call to confirm the instruction.

Orion’s bank notices that the transaction is unusual but processes it after the finance director confirms the payment. The money passes through accounts held at three banks. Some funds are converted into cryptocurrency, some are used to buy a London apartment, and £400,000 is transferred to an innocent supplier in satisfaction of a genuine debt owed by one of the fraudsters.

Advise Orion, its bank, the recipient banks and recipients on payment authority, contractual duties, negligence, unjust enrichment, change of position, knowing receipt where relevant, tracing, proprietary claims, freezing relief and practical recovery.

## Question 19 — 4,000 words — Essay — Competition and Digital Markets Law

“A dominant digital platform may harm competition without raising prices, because its control over data, ranking, interoperability and market access can be more important than conventional pricing power.”

Critically assess this statement under the applicable UK competition and digital-markets regimes.

Consider market definition, dominance, self-preferencing, tying, discriminatory access, exclusive arrangements, predatory conduct, data advantages, merger control, consumer harm, innovation and public and private enforcement.

## Question 20 — 4,000 words — Problem — Medical Law

Leila is admitted to hospital with a serious but treatable condition. She has a history of fluctuating mental capacity. During a period of apparent confusion, she refuses a recommended operation.

Her family insists that she previously said she would accept any treatment necessary to remain alive. The treating doctor proceeds without conducting a formal capacity assessment. A newly introduced AI diagnostic system recommends a particular surgical technique, but the doctor does not tell Leila that the system has a significant error rate for patients with her characteristics.

The operation causes a recognised complication. Evidence suggests that Leila might have chosen a different treatment had the risks and alternatives been explained.

Advise the parties on capacity, consent, best interests, emergency treatment, disclosure of risks, clinical negligence, causation, confidentiality and the significance of reliance on AI.

## Question 21 — 5,000 words — Problem — Public Procurement and Administrative Law

A government department launches a £2 billion procurement for a national digital identity system. Alpha Ltd and Beta Ltd reach the final stage.

Internal departmental emails show that officials considered Alpha technically superior but were concerned that awarding the contract to Alpha would attract political criticism because one of its shareholders had publicly opposed government policy.

Beta receives the contract. Journalists later reveal that one member of the evaluation panel previously worked as a consultant for Beta. The department also changed an important scoring criterion after final tenders were submitted without informing Alpha.

Six months of implementation work has already begun, and Beta has entered into substantial subcontracts.

Advise Alpha, the department and Beta on procurement obligations, public-law review, procedural fairness, bias, improper purpose, rationality, proportionality, standing, time limits, interim relief, damages and the consequences of disturbing an operational contract.

## Question 22 — 5,000 words — Essay — Environmental and Climate Law

“Climate litigation forces courts to choose between enforcing legal accountability and entering areas of social and economic policy for which judges lack democratic legitimacy and institutional competence.”

Critically evaluate this proposition.

Consider statutory duties, government climate policies, standing, justiciability, rationality, proportionality, human rights, causation, scientific uncertainty, intergenerational interests, corporate disclosure, remedies and the constitutional limits of judicial intervention.

## Question 23 — 5,000 words — Problem — Data Protection and Privacy

A retailer combines customer purchase histories, location data, social-media information and data purchased from brokers. It uses the information to infer customers’ health conditions, financial vulnerability and likely pregnancy status.

The retailer shares risk scores with an insurer and an employer. The insurer automatically increases some customers’ premiums. The employer rejects certain applicants without human review.

A cyberattack subsequently exposes the profiles of 300,000 people. The retailer delayed announcing the breach because it feared reputational damage. Several individuals request copies of their data and explanations of the scoring system, but the retailer refuses on the basis that the model is commercially confidential.

Advise the retailer, insurer, employer and affected individuals under the applicable data-protection, privacy and equality frameworks. Address lawful processing, transparency, special-category data, profiling, automated decisions, security, breach notification, access rights, compensation and regulatory enforcement.

## Question 24 — 5,000 words — Essay — Legal Ethics and Artificial Intelligence

“The use of generative AI by lawyers does not create entirely new professional duties; it merely increases the risk that existing duties of competence, confidentiality, supervision and candour will be breached.”

Critically assess this statement.

Consider duties to clients, duties to the court, verification of authorities, misleading submissions, confidentiality, legal professional privilege, data security, supervision of junior lawyers and external technology providers, discrimination, explainability, billing, access to justice and responsibility for AI-generated work.

## Question 25 — 5,000 words — Problem — Insolvency and Corporate Transactions

Omega Ltd is part of a corporate group experiencing serious financial difficulty. Six months before entering administration, Omega:

- repays a loan owed to a company controlled by its managing director;
- transfers machinery worth £3 million to its parent company for £500,000;
- grants a floating charge to secure previously unsecured borrowing;
- continues accepting customer deposits despite serious doubts that orders can be completed; and
- pays one essential supplier in full while leaving other suppliers unpaid.

The directors argue that they genuinely believed that a rescue investment would be obtained. A bank claims fixed and floating security over Omega’s assets. A supplier asserts a retention-of-title clause, while another creditor seeks to set off mutual debts.

Advise the administrator, directors, secured creditors, suppliers and customers on creditor priorities, fixed and floating charges, transactions at an undervalue, preferences, invalid floating charges, wrongful and fraudulent trading, misfeasance, creditor interests, set-off and retention of title.

## Question 26 — 6,000 words — Problem — Construction and Commercial Law

City Developments Ltd appoints BuildCo Ltd to design and construct a mixed-use tower for £180 million. The contract provides for phased completion, liquidated damages, extensions of time, performance security and adjudication.

BuildCo appoints specialist subcontractors. During construction:

- the employer repeatedly changes the design;
- an unexpected ground condition causes delay;
- the façade system fails fire-safety testing;
- the structural engineer approves a design later found to be defective;
- a subcontractor becomes insolvent;
- the employer refuses to certify payment;
- BuildCo suspends work; and
- a performance bond is called.

The building opens eighteen months late. Apartments are sold before serious water-ingress and fire-safety defects are discovered. The employer claims replacement costs, lost rent and reputational loss. BuildCo argues that the employer’s variations caused most of the delay and that the alleged losses are excluded.

Advise the employer, contractor, engineer, subcontractors, purchasers, insurers and bond provider. Address contractual interpretation, design responsibility, variations, delay, extensions of time, prevention, liquidated damages, defects, causation, remoteness, economic loss, contribution, insurance, adjudication, limitation and remedies.

## Question 27 — 7,000 words — Essay — Constitutional and Administrative Law

“The modern constitution of the United Kingdom is no longer adequately explained by parliamentary sovereignty alone.”

Critically evaluate this statement.

Your answer should analyse parliamentary sovereignty, the rule of law, constitutional statutes, judicial review, prerogative powers, devolution, retained and assimilated legal frameworks where relevant, human rights, ouster clauses, access to courts, ministerial accountability and the relationship between legal and political constitutionalism.

Consider whether recent constitutional developments represent genuine legal limits on Parliament or merely political constraints that Parliament may ultimately override.

## Question 28 — 8,000 words — Problem — Land, Trusts, Family Property and Insolvency

Harriet purchases a large house in her sole name. Her partner, Marcus, contributes 35% of the deposit and pays for extensive renovations. Harriet tells Marcus that the property is “our permanent family home,” but no declaration of trust is signed.

Harriet later obtains a mortgage by falsely stating that she is the sole occupier. Marcus is living at the property when the mortgage is completed.

Harriet grants part of the property to a business tenant under an informal seven-year agreement. A neighbour claims a right of way used for twenty years. Harriet’s adult daughter occupies an annex and claims that Harriet promised it would eventually belong to her.

Harriet secretly transfers the house to a company she controls. The company grants a charge to a lender and subsequently enters insolvency proceedings. Harriet and Marcus separate, and disputes arise concerning their children’s continued occupation.

Advise all relevant parties on express, resulting and constructive trusts, proprietary estoppel, actual occupation, overriding interests, mortgage priority, undue influence where relevant, leases, easements, registration, transactions defrauding creditors, insolvency, family occupation and remedies.

## Question 29 — 9,000 words — Problem — Corporate Fraud, Regulation and Litigation

GlobalTech plc operates through subsidiaries in the United Kingdom, Europe, Asia and the Middle East. An internal whistleblower alleges that senior managers:

- paid bribes through overseas consultants;
- disguised the payments as marketing expenses;
- breached financial sanctions;
- inflated revenue by recording non-existent sales;
- concealed major product-safety defects;
- misused customer data; and
- retaliated against employees who raised concerns.

The board commissions an internal investigation. Some directors want the investigation controlled entirely by the company’s usual solicitors, while others fear that documents and interviews may later have to be disclosed.

After journalists reveal part of the allegations, the company’s share price collapses. Regulators begin investigations, shareholders threaten proceedings, customers terminate contracts and lenders consider accelerating outstanding loans. One subsidiary is close to insolvency.

Advise the company, directors, employees, shareholders, creditors and relevant third parties.

Address corporate and individual criminal liability, bribery, fraud, sanctions, false accounting, directors’ duties, whistleblowing, employment retaliation, privilege, internal investigations, disclosure, regulatory cooperation, derivative claims, securities-related claims, contractual termination, insolvency, preservation of evidence, interim remedies and litigation strategy.

## Question 30 — 10,000 words — Problem — Multi-Area AI Platform Litigation

JusticeAI plc operates an AI platform used by solicitors, insurers, employers, banks and local authorities. The system generates legal assessments and risk scores using customer-uploaded documents, licensed databases, web-scraped material and information purchased from data brokers.

A law firm relies on JusticeAI when advising a client that a valuable claim is outside the limitation period. The advice is wrong, and the client loses the opportunity to pursue litigation allegedly worth £8 million.

An insurer uses JusticeAI to classify applicants as high-risk. Statistical analysis later shows substantially higher adverse classifications for applicants belonging to particular protected groups.

A bank uses the platform to identify suspicious customers and closes hundreds of accounts without providing detailed explanations.

A local authority uses JusticeAI to identify suspected benefit fraud and automatically suspends payments without first allowing affected households to respond.

An employer uses the system to shortlist candidates. Applicants with disabilities and applicants over the age of fifty receive significantly lower scores.

JusticeAI later discovers that an important source database contained systematically inaccurate information. Internal emails show that senior engineers warned directors six months earlier that the platform produced unreliable results in approximately 8% of complex cases. Marketing nevertheless continued to describe the system as having “near-human professional accuracy.”

A former employee alleges that parts of the model architecture and training materials were copied from software belonging to her previous employer. Several copyright owners also allege that JusticeAI reproduces protected material in its outputs.

Following media coverage:

- customers terminate their contracts;
- individuals bring claims;
- regulators begin investigations;
- the company’s share price collapses;
- shareholders consider proceedings against the directors;
- lenders question whether JusticeAI remains solvent; and
- the company attempts to delete some internal testing records.

Advise all relevant parties.

Your answer should integrate, where relevant:

contract law; misrepresentation; negligence; professional reliance; causation; loss of a chance; remoteness; exclusion clauses; consumer protection; public law; procedural fairness; equality law; data protection; confidentiality; intellectual property; directors’ duties; shareholder remedies; insolvency; regulatory enforcement; preservation and disclosure of evidence; privilege; interim remedies; damages; and overall litigation strategy.

The answer must identify:

1. every significant claimant and defendant;
2. the strongest and weakest causes of action;
3. overlapping and alternative claims;
4. factual matters requiring further evidence;
5. possible defences;
6. causation and quantification difficulties;
7. public-law and private-law remedies;
8. regulatory and insolvency consequences; and
9. a prioritised litigation and settlement strategy.

## Standard bot-training requirements

For every problem question, require the bot to follow:

**Party map → material facts → legal issues → governing rules and authorities → application → counterarguments → missing evidence → liability conclusion → remedies → procedure and strategy**

For every essay question, require:

**Define the proposition → identify the orthodox legal position → present supporting arguments → present competing arguments → analyse authorities and policy → evaluate practical operation → reach a reasoned conclusion**

The bot should also be required to:

- distinguish facts from assumptions;
- avoid inventing cases, legislation or quotations;
- identify uncertain or developing areas of law;
- address both sides rather than giving a one-sided answer;
- discuss remedies and procedure, not merely substantive liability;
- maintain consistent facts and conclusions throughout long answers; and
- comply with the specified word count rather than padding the response with repeated law.
