# Fiduciary duties
- The core of fiduciary duty is loyalty (Bristol and West BS v Mothew [1998] Ch 1, Millett LJ). Commentators debate whether "loyalty" or the orientation of judgment is the better description; the rules are protective and prophylactic.
- No-conflict rule: a fiduciary must not, without authority, place themselves where interest and duty conflict (Bray v Ford [1896] AC 44). It is strict. Sub-rules:
- No-profit rule: a fiduciary must account for any unauthorised profit made from the position, whether or not the principal lost anything (Keech v Sandford (1726); Regal (Hastings) v Gulliver [1942/1967]; Boardman v Phipps [1967] 2 AC 46, where a solicitor and beneficiary who acted in good faith still had to account for profits from information gained while acting for the trust).
- Remedies: rescission for self-dealing or unfair dealing; account of profits, personal or proprietary. Bribes and secret commissions are held on constructive trust for the principal (A-G for Hong Kong v Reid [1994] 1 AC 324; FHR European Ventures v Cedar Capital [2014] UKSC 45). Companies may recover directors' unauthorised profits even where the directors acted criminally (CPS v Aquila Advisory [2021] UKSC 49).
- Revision links: distributional certainty (Boyce v Boyce fails where the chooser can no longer choose; Re Knapton cured by an order of choice); administrative unworkability applies only to discretionary trusts, where the class is so hopelessly wide it is not "anything like a class" (McPhail v Doulton; R v District Auditor ex p West Yorkshire MCC).
