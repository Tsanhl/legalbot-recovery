## Definitions
- **Scheme types.** Pension Schemes Act 1993, s 1 defines "occupational pension scheme" and "personal pension scheme".
- Occupational schemes are regulated mainly by the Pension Schemes Act 1993, the Pensions Acts 1995 and 2004, and the Pension Schemes Act 2021.
- Personal pensions are regulated under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000.
- Tax registration is governed separately by the Finance Act 2004 (ss 150–151).
- **Master trusts** (multi-employer money purchase schemes) require authorisation under the Pension Schemes Act 2017.
- money purchase benefits (Pension Schemes Act 1993, s 181; Pensions Act 2011, s 29);
- collective money purchase benefits (Pension Schemes Act 2021, Part 1);
- **Member categories.** Pensions Act 1995, s 124 and Pensions Act 2004, s 318 define active, deferred and pensioner members. Finance Act 2004, s 151 treats survivors as pensioner members.
- **Contracting out.** It was abolished for DB schemes from 6 April 2016 (Pensions Act 2014). Guaranteed minimum pensions accrued between 1978 and 1997, and section 9(2B) rights between 1997 and 2016, remain.
- **Minimum pension age.** The normal minimum pension age rises from 55 to 57 on 6 April 2028 (Finance Act 2022).

## Why irrevocable trusts
- **Trustee Act 2000.** Most of the Act is disapplied to occupational pension schemes (s 36).
- **Investment liability.** Liability for breach of investment duties cannot be excluded (Pensions Act 1995, s 33). Otherwise, trustee exemption clauses can be valid (Armitage v Nurse [1998] Ch 241).

## EU-derived law and Brexit
- **Origins.** The IORP II Directive (EU) 2016/2341, transposed mainly through the Pensions Act 2004; the employer insolvency directive (2008/94/EC); equal treatment law; and TUPE.
- **Status now.** Retained EU law became "assimilated law" under the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023.

## Tax (outline)
- **Contributions.** Employer and employee contributions attract relief (Finance Act 2004, Part 4).
- **Tax-free lump sum** of up to 25%, subject to the lump sum allowance of £268,275 after the lifetime allowance was abolished from 6 April 2024 (Finance Act 2024).

## Relevant general law
- **Third-party rights.** Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999, s 6(3) excludes enforcement of certain terms of employment contracts against employees. Survivors typically enforce rights as trust beneficiaries.
- **Overpayments.** Recovery is subject to change of position (Scottish Equitable v Derby [2001] EWCA Civ 369).
- **The employer's implied duty of good faith** in exercising scheme powers: Imperial Group Pension Trust v Imperial Tobacco [1991] 1 WLR 589; Mihlenstedt v Barclays Bank [1989] IRLR 522.
- **Rationality in contractual discretions:** Braganza v BP Shipping [2015] UKSC 17.
- failure to consider relevant, or consideration of irrelevant, matters (Pitt v Holt [2013] UKSC 26).
- **Unfunded arrangements.** Granada Group v Law Debenture Trust [2016] EWCA Civ 1289 concerned an unfunded scheme.
