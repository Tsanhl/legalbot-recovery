## Provisions
- **Article 49 TFEU.** It prohibits restrictions on establishment by nationals of one Member State in another. This covers:
- **Article 51 TFEU.** An exception for activities connected, even occasionally, with the exercise of official authority.
- **Article 52 TFEU.** Derogations on grounds of public policy, security and health.
- **Article 54 TFEU.** Companies formed under Member State law and with their registered office, central administration or principal place of business in the EU are treated like natural persons.

## Personal scope
- **Self-employed or worker?** Self-employed persons fall under Article 49, not Article 45. Self-employed prostitution was treated as an economic activity in Jany (C-268/99).
- Services under Article 56 are temporary, judged by duration, regularity, periodicity and continuity. A service provider may still have infrastructure such as an office (Gebhard, C-55/94).
- **Companies.** Each Member State defines the connecting factor for incorporation, following either the incorporation theory or the real seat theory (Cartesio, C-210/06). Article 54 accepts both.
- **Wholly internal situations** are excluded. The Court finds a cross-border link readily, for example a national using a foreign LLM title at home (Kraus, C-19/92).

## Content of the right
- **Movement and family.** The rights to leave, enter and reside, and to bring family members, come from Directive 2004/38 for natural persons. Self-employed status is retained on the same terms as for workers (Article 7(3)).
- It is justifiable only under Article 52 (read narrowly, and with Directive 2004/38), proportionately and consistently with fundamental rights.
- The official authority exception is also read narrowly. It covers only activities with a direct and specific connection to official authority. The Belgian nationality requirement for the Bar failed in Reyners (2/74).
- Examples: a prior-residence condition for maternity allowances (Commission v Luxembourg, C-111/91) and the non-recognition of foreign qualifications. In Vlassopoulou (C-340/89) the host state must compare foreign qualifications with its own requirements.
- **Tax and social advantages.** No provision mirrors Article 7(2) of Regulation 492/2011, but Article 49 prohibits discrimination in:
- business premises (Steinhauser, 197/84);
- social housing and subsidised mortgages (Commission v Italy, 63/86).
- See also Article 24(1) of Directive 2004/38.
- **More than one establishment.** Keeping chambers in Germany while practising in Paris was protected (Klopp, 107/83).

## Non-discriminatory barriers
- **CaixaBank (C-442/02).** A French ban on paying interest on current accounts hindered foreign-owned subsidiaries, which needed price competition to attract deposits against incumbents with extensive branch networks. It was a restriction.
- **Limit.** Effects that are too uncertain and indirect are not caught, such as Sunday closing (Semeraro Casa Uno, C-418/93).
