## Quick frameworks
- **Theft (Theft Act 1968, s 1).**
- Actus reus: appropriation (s 3: any assumption of an owner's rights, even with consent: Gomez); property (s 4); belonging to another (s 5).
- Mens rea: dishonesty (s 2, then Ivey v Genting [2017] UKSC 67 as applied in Barton and Booth [2020] EWCA Crim 575: D's actual belief about the facts, then the standards of ordinary decent people); intention to permanently deprive (s 6).
- "Borrowing" and later keeping: a later assumption of rights is itself an appropriation, and s 6 covers treating the thing as one's own to dispose of.
- **Rape (Sexual Offences Act 2003, s 1).**
- Actus reus: penile penetration of the vagina, anus or mouth, and the absence of consent (consider s 74 freedom and capacity here, and the ss 75–76 presumptions, most serious first).
- Mens rea: intentional penetration and no reasonable belief in consent (s 1(2), taking account of all the circumstances).
- **Self-defence.** Common law, the Criminal Law Act 1967, s 3 and the Criminal Justice and Immigration Act 2008, s 76 (see the self-defence note).

## General principles
- **Innocent agency.** D is the principal where D acts through an agent who is not criminally responsible. In Michael (1840) 9 C & P 356, the nurse and the five-year-old child were innocent agents in the poisoning.
