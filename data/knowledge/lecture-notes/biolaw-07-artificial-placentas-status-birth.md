## The technology
- Lamb trials succeeded. The proposed human target population is 22 to 24 weeks (Partridge et al, 2017).
- **Timeline and hurdles.** The US FDA Paediatric Advisory Committee (2023) discussed possible human use within about a decade. Hurdles remain: small samples, short durations, mixed results, and animal physiology unlike humans.

## Birth and legal personality
- Paton v BPAS [1979] QB 276: a fetus has no rights before birth.
- Re F (In Utero) [1988] Fam 122: no wardship of a fetus, protecting the pregnant woman.
- Complete birth (R v Crutchley (1837)) and being born alive.
- The Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953 treats breathing or any other sign of life as registrable.
- C v S [1988] QB 135: construed with the registration legislation. Breathing, naturally or with a ventilator, counts.
- Rance v Mid-Downs HA [1991] 1 QB 587: living through its own lungs without connection to the mother.
- R v Enoch (1833): independent circulation.
- effects on abortion law through the Infant Life (Preservation) Act 1929, which turns on a fetus "capable of being born alive".

## Seminar problem types
- **Medical error injuring a gestateling.** Recourse depends on its legal status. If it is born alive, compare the Congenital Disabilities (Civil Liability) Act 1976. Also consider the parents' own claims and the consent and experimental-treatment framework.
- Legal motherhood rests on gestation (HFEA 2008, s 33), so with no gestating woman the law has a gap.
