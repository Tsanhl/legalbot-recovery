# Creating express trusts: formalities and secret trusts
- Inter vivos formalities: trusts of personal property need no formality (oral is enough). A declaration of trust of land must be evidenced by signed writing (LPA 1925, s 53(1)(b)); this does not apply to resulting or constructive trusts (s 53(2)).
- Equity will not allow a statute to be used as an instrument of fraud: where land is transferred on an oral understanding that it will be held on trust, the transferee cannot rely on the lack of writing (Rochefoucauld v Boustead [1897]). This applies to property transferred by the beneficiary to the trustee, not to property the putative trustee buys from a third party and then orally declares a trust over (Re Smith [2023] EWHC 255 (Comm)).
- Testamentary trusts must meet the Wills Act 1837, s 9 (writing, signed, two attesting witnesses).
- Fully secret trust (the will shows an absolute gift): intention to impose a legally binding (not merely moral) obligation (Re Snowden [1979]), communication to and acceptance by the legatee before death. They are hard to prove without evidence (Re Mattingley [2021] EWHC 3353 (Ch)). If the trust fails the legatee takes beneficially; if the legatee predeceases, the trust fails.
