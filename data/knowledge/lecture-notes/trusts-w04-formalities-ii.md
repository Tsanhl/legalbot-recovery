## Policy limits on trusts
- Sham or illusory trusts are disregarded (JSC Mezhdunarodniy Promyshlenniy Bank v Pugachev [2017] EWHC 2426 (Ch)).
- Illegality: the reliance approach of Tinsley v Milligan [1994] was replaced by Patel v Mirza [2016] UKSC 42, which asks about the purpose of the prohibition breached, other relevant public policies, and whether denying the claim would be a proportionate response.

## Dispositions of equitable interests (LPA 1925, s 53(1)(c))
- A disposition of a subsisting equitable interest must be in signed writing (or by will). The equitable interest is the beneficiary's right in the property, not the property itself.
- Key scenarios:
- A beneficiary orally directing trustees to hold for someone else is a disposition needing writing (Grey v IRC [1960]).
- A beneficiary declaring themselves sub-trustee: whether this is a disposition depends on whether they retain active duties.
- A specifically enforceable contract to assign an equitable interest raises a constructive trust, but the courts still required writing for the transfer (Oughtred v IRC [1960]).
- Transferring legal and equitable title together on the beneficiary's direction needs no separate writing (Vandervell v IRC [1967]).
- Summary of formalities: inter vivos express trusts of personalty can be created orally; trusts of land must be evidenced in signed writing; dispositions of any equitable interest must be in signed writing; testamentary trusts must satisfy the Wills Act.
