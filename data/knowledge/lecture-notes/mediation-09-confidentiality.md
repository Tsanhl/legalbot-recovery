## Common law protections
- English courts have indicated that a distinct mediation privilege may develop (see Farm Assist v DEFRA (No 2) [2009] EWHC 1102 (TCC), where the mediator was compelled but confidentiality was recognised).

## Contract
- Unilever v Procter & Gamble [2000] 1 WLR 2436 (CA) suggested this is possible in principle.
- Union Carbide Canada v Bombardier (SCC 2014) allows it only by clear terms, which were absent there.

## Fraud and crime
- **England.** Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, s 328, being concerned in an arrangement that facilitates criminal property is an offence unless an authorised disclosure is made. A disclosure that is not required may expose the mediator to suit.

## Statute
- **EU Mediation Directive, Article 7.**
- Article 9: information given in caucus may be disclosed to the other party unless its provider asks for it to stay confidential. This is an "open communication" default.
- Article 10: all information about the mediation is confidential unless disclosure is required by law or for implementing or enforcing a settlement.
- Article 11: broad inadmissibility in disclosure and evidence.
- The California Evidence Code goes further: caucus information stays confidential unless its provider consents, and there is a broad duty over the whole process.
- Rojas v Superior Court (Cal 2004): confidentiality of material prepared for mediation is effectively absolute, subject to narrow statutory exceptions.
