# Mortgages
- Creation: a legal mortgage requires a deed (LPA 1925, s 52) and, in registered land, registration (LRA 2002, s 27). An equitable mortgage arises from a mortgage of an equitable interest, or a failed legal mortgage where there is a specifically enforceable contract (LP(MP)A 1989, s 2).

## Protecting the mortgagee: four rights and remedies
- **Possession**: available "before the ink is dry" unless excluded (Four-Maids v Dudley Marshall [1957]), in practice by court proceedings. Limits:
- it must be sought in good faith to protect the security (Quennell v Maltby [1979]);
- a mortgagee in possession must account for income it received or should have received (White v City of London Brewery (1889)) and take reasonable care of the property;
- the inherent power to adjourn is only for short periods of about 28 days (Birmingham Citizens PBS v Caunt [1962]);
- for dwelling-houses, the Administration of Justice Act 1970, s 36 lets the court adjourn or postpone if the mortgagor is likely to pay the sums due within a reasonable period. "Reasonable period" is presumptively the rest of the mortgage term (Cheltenham & Gloucester BS v Norgan [1996]); "sums due" means arrears, not the whole loan (AJA 1973, s 8); s 36 applies only once the lender has started proceedings (Ropaigealach v Barclays Bank [2000]); it is not available where the sale would leave negative equity (Cheltenham & Gloucester v Krausz [1997]).
- **Sale**: the power must have arisen and be exercisable (LPA 1925, ss 101, 103). The mortgagee must act in good faith and take reasonable care to obtain the true market value at the time it chooses to sell (Cuckmere Brick v Mutual Finance [1971]); it need not wait for the market to improve, but must market properly (Predeth v Castle Phillips [1986]); it need not pursue planning permission (Silven Properties v RBS [2003]). A sale to an associate requires proof of a fair price (Tse Kwong Lam v Wong Chit Sen [1983]). Breach makes the mortgagee liable for the shortfall or the sale may be set aside.
- The mortgagor can sell with consent or by court order (LPA 1925, s 91): ordered to stop spiralling debt (Palk v Mortgage Services Funding [1993]) and even for non-financial reasons despite a shortfall (Polonski v Lloyds Bank Mortgages [1998]).
