## Origins and remaining role
- **Industrialisation and urbanisation** made nuisance a way of balancing neighbours' competing land uses. St Helen's Smelting Co v Tipping (1865) concerned industrial fumes damaging vegetation.
- Dennis v Ministry of Defence [2003] EWHC 793 (QB): jet noise, with damages awarded instead of an injunction;
- Coventry v Lawrence [2014] UKSC 13: motor sport noise.
- **Limited by statutory schemes.** Where a statutory scheme exists, nuisance is displaced: Marcic v Thames Water [2003] UKHL 66 (sewerage).
- **Statute alongside:** statutory nuisance under the Environmental Protection Act 1990, Part III, and the Environment Act 2021 framework.

## Possibilities
- Halsey v Esso Petroleum [1961] 1 WLR 683: acid smuts, smell and noise from a depot;
- Cambridge Water v Eastern Counties Leather [1994] 2 AC 264: groundwater contamination, where foreseeability of the type of damage was required.
- **A flexible standard.** "Reasonable user" between neighbours is flexible and accessible. Foreseeability governs liability for hazards the defendant did not create (Sedleigh-Denfield v O'Callaghan [1940] AC 880; Leakey v National Trust [1980] QB 485), but nuisance does not import the whole of negligence.
- Coventry v Lawrence loosened the Shelfer v City of London Electric Lighting [1895] 1 Ch 287 criteria for awarding damages instead of an injunction. Whether Shelfer survives in full is debated.

## Limitations
- **Anthropocentric.** Only the claimant's own protected interests in land can be vindicated (Hunter v Canary Wharf [1997] AC 655: a proprietary interest is needed). Nature itself has no standing.
- Courts have relaxed causation before (Fairchild v Glenhaven [2002] UKHL 22 for mesothelioma).
- Whether they will do so for climate claims is open. Compare Smith v Fonterra [2024] NZSC 5, which allowed a climate tort claim to go to trial.
