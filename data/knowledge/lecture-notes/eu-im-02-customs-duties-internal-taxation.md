## Customs union (Article 28 TFEU)
- **Internal dimension.** No customs duties on goods moving between Member States.
- **External dimension.** A common customs tariff on goods from outside the EU.
- **Free circulation.** Third-country goods in free circulation are treated like EU goods.

## Article 30 TFEU: customs duties and charges having equivalent effect (CEEs)
- **What a CEE is.** Any pecuniary charge, however named or applied, that is imposed unilaterally on goods because they cross a frontier (Bresciani, C-87/75).
- **Internal borders too.** The prohibition applies to charges at internal borders within one Member State (Carbonati Apuani, C-72/03).
- **No justification.** The charge's purpose is irrelevant. Using the revenue for a social fund does not save it (Diamantarbeiders, 2 and 3/69).
- **Two exceptions only.**
- **Consideration for a genuine service** that specifically benefits the individual trader. The statistical levy did not qualify (Commission v Italy, 24/68; see also Commission v Belgium (Warehousing), 132/82).
- **Fees for inspections required by EU law** (Commission v Germany, 18/87), on four conditions:
- the fee does not exceed the actual cost;
- the inspection is mandatory and uniform across the EU;
- it is prescribed by EU law;
- it promotes the free movement of goods.

## Article 110 TFEU: discriminatory internal taxation
- **Scope.** General internal taxes applied to domestic and imported goods alike. Member States keep their tax competence, subject to two limits.
- **Article 110(1): similar products.**
- Similarity depends on objective characteristics (content, method of production, price) and on whether the products meet similar consumer needs.
- Both direct and indirect discrimination are caught. Humblot (112/84) is the example: a car tax that jumped steeply above a power band that only imported cars exceeded.
- Indirect differentiation may be justified by objective criteria pursued compatibly with EU law (Chemial Farmaceutici, 140/79: synthetic versus fermentation alcohol).
- The remedy is equal taxation.
- **Article 110(2): competing products.**
- Where products are not similar but are in partial or potential competition, the tax must not afford indirect protection to domestic products.
- In Commission v UK (Wine and Beer), 170/78, the Court looked at substitutability over time and across markets, not just at existing national habits. Tax policy must not crystallise consumer habits so as to protect domestic industry.
- Method: ask about similarity first. If the products are not similar, ask whether they compete. If they do, ask whether the tax is protective.

## Article 30 and Article 110 are mutually exclusive
- **No domestic product.** A charge that forms part of a general internal tax system is still assessed under Article 110 even where there is no domestic comparator (Co-Frutta, 193/85).
- **Parafiscal charges** (a general levy whose proceeds benefit domestic producers):
- if the proceeds fully offset the burden on domestic goods, the charge is a CEE under Article 30;
- if they only partly offset it, it is discriminatory taxation under Article 110.
