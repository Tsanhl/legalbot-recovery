## Problem-question method
- State the law (for example, s 14(2), with the test in s 14(2A) and factors in s 14(2B)).

## Quality, fitness and sample (SGA 1979)
- s 14(2): in a sale in the course of a business, goods must be of satisfactory quality: the standard a reasonable person would regard as satisfactory, taking account of description, price and other relevant circumstances (s 14(2A)), with factors such as fitness for common purposes, appearance and finish, freedom from minor defects, safety and durability (s 14(2B)). Not covered: defects drawn to the buyer's attention, or which examination actually made ought to have revealed (s 14(2C)).
- s 14(3): fitness for a particular purpose made known, unless the buyer did not rely, or it was unreasonable to rely, on the seller's skill (Jewson v Kelly [2003]; Ashington Piggeries).
- s 15: sale by sample (the parties must intend it: Godley v Perry [1960]); the bulk must correspond with the sample and be free of defects not apparent on reasonable examination of it.
- Buyer's loss of the right to reject: waiver (s 11(2)) or acceptance (s 11(4)).

## Excluding implied terms
- s 12 cannot be excluded. ss 13–15 may be excluded against a business buyer only if reasonable (UCTA 1977, ss 6, 11, with the Schedule 2 guidelines). Against consumers the CRA 2015 duties (ss 9–17) cannot be excluded.

## Nemo dat table (who relies on what)
- Original owner: nemo dat (s 21).
- End buyer: court-ordered sale (s 21(2)(b)); mercantile agent (Factors Act 1889, s 2), which requires consent to possession in an agent-connected capacity (Astley Industrial Trust v Miller [1968]), sale in business hours at a proper place (Oppenheimer), and good faith, judged against the buyer's own trade experience (Summers v Havard [2011]: an undervalue sale to a car dealer gave notice); voidable title (s 23); seller in possession (s 24); buyer in possession (s 25); private purchaser of a hire-purchase vehicle (HPA 1964, s 27). Title to stolen goods can never be obtained (National Employers v Jones).

## Frustration checklist
- Specific goods: s 6 (before the contract) or s 7 (after, before risk passes); otherwise common law. Consequences at common law: discharge for the future, recovery only on total failure of consideration (Fibrosa; Whincup v Hughes (1871)), entire-contract rule (Appleby v Myers); otherwise the 1943 Act, ss 1(2)–(3).
- Pre-contract non-existence depends on what was agreed: seller warranted existence (McRae), buyer took a chance (Couturier), condition precedent (AJB v Credit du Nord), or common mistake at the heart of the contract (Bell v Lever Bros).

## Passing-of-property checklist
- Specific goods are identified in the contract (serial numbers: Hendy Lennox v Grahame Puttick [1984]; the buyer pointing to goods in the store); unascertained goods are described generically.
- ss 16–18 and 20A–20B. Ascertainment by exhaustion (Wait & James v Midland Bank (1926)). A single buyer purchasing a whole bulk in separate contracts can acquire property (The Elafi).
