## Structure of a proprietary claim
- Rationale debate: Foskett v McKeown [2001] 1 AC 102 treated tracing as vindicating property rights (beneficiaries whose money paid two of five premiums took a proportionate 40% share of the policy proceeds); critics argue the true basis is reversing unjust enrichment.

## Remedies
- Equity: the asset or a proportionate share, an equitable lien securing the misappropriated value, or subrogation (Boscawen v Bajwa [1996] 1 WLR 328).

## Common law versus equity
- Common law traces only through clean substitutions, not mixed funds (Lipkin Gorman v Karpnale [1991] 2 AC 548; Agip (Africa) v Jackson [1991] Ch 547; FC Jones v Jones [1997] Ch 159).
- Equity traces through mixtures but has been said to require a fiduciary relationship (Re Diplock [1948]; Chase Manhattan v Israel-British Bank [1981]).

## Rules for mixed bank accounts
- Where a wrongdoer mixes trust money with their own, the trustee is presumed to spend their own money first (Re Hallett's Estate (1880)); but if they buy an asset and then dissipate the rest, the beneficiary can claim the asset (Re Oatway [1903]); the claimant may choose the more valuable route (Shalson v Russo [2005] Ch 281).
- Lowest intermediate balance: no claim beyond the lowest balance between deposit and claim (James Roscoe v Winder [1915]).
- Paying off a debt usually dissipates the money, although backwards tracing is possible where the transactions were coordinated (Federal Republic of Brazil v Durant [2015] UKPC 35).
