## The choice of law process
- The court places the issue in a legal category using English law's categories, derived from authority and principle, applied "in a broad internationalist spirit" (Raiffeisen Zentralbank v Five Star Trading [2001] EWCA Civ 68 at [27]).
- Macmillan v Bishopsgate (No 3) [1996] 1 WLR 387, 405: the reference to New York law as the situs of shares was to domestic law, and the renvoi argument had been abandoned. Supporters argue that a judgment contrary to the view of the situs is likely to be ineffective.
- With renvoi, the answer depends on which law a Chinese court would apply. Chinese law excludes renvoi (Law on the Application of Laws to Foreign-related Civil Relationships 2010, art 9) and applies the law of the movable's location when the legal fact occurred (art 37; Civil Code 2020, art 311).

## Forum non conveniens: Spiliada Maritime v Cansulex [1987] AC 460
- **Background.** It developed from The Atlantic Star. Spiliada was a service-out case, but Lord Goff set common principles for both stays and service out.
- A sulphur cargo was shipped from Vancouver to India. England won because Staughton J was already hearing a similar case (the "Cambridgeshire" factor).
- **Procedure.** Application for a stay under CPR Part 11. The action is held in abeyance and may be revived if an undertaking is broken.
- **Limb 1** (burden on the defendant): there is another available forum that is clearly or distinctly more appropriate. That means the forum with which the action has the most real and substantial connection (VTB Capital v Nutritek [2013] UKSC 5; Unwired Planet v Huawei [2020] UKSC 37 at [94]).
- **Availability.** The defendant must be subject to the foreign court's jurisdiction, by presence, prior submission or an undertaking to submit.
- **Comparative factors:**
- where the parties reside. Residence acquired for tactical reasons carries less weight. Suing a parent company can anchor a case in England (Lungowe v Vedanta [2019] UKSC 20);
- evidence and witnesses, including translation and compellability. The place of a tort is weighty if its law also applies. Remote evidence has lessened this factor;
- governing law. An English choice of law is weighty. Foreign law is not always difficult, and the foreign court might apply a different law;
- related proceedings (lis alibi pendens). The "Cambridgeshire" factor has rarely succeeded since. The risk of fragmented complex litigation can outweigh even an English jurisdiction clause (Donohue v Armco [2001] UKHL 64).
- **The overall shape of the litigation matters,** including the likely defence. Claimants can frame claims selectively, for example suing only the English parent over asbestos mining in South Africa (Lubbe v Cape plc [2000] 1 WLR 1545).
- **Limb 2** (burden on the claimant, by clear and cogent evidence (The Abidin Daver [1984] AC 398)): justice nevertheless requires that the case stay in England.
- Minor advantages carry little weight: higher damages, fuller disclosure, interest.
- Significant factors: legal aid or funding available only in England, extreme delay (especially in personal injury), disproportionate costs, and the inability of the foreign court to join all parties, risking inconsistent judgments.
- **Time bars.** If the claim is time-barred abroad, a stay may be refused unless the claimant is to blame (Spiliada at 483). The Foreign Limitation Periods Act 1984 applies the lex causae's limitation rules. Where there is an exclusive foreign jurisdiction clause, the claimant is usually at fault for not suing in time there.
- **Different outcomes abroad** are normally irrelevant, unless the foreign court would ignore the parties' choice of law.
- **Quality of the foreign court.** A real risk that justice will not be obtained through incompetence, lack of independence or corruption can suffice (AK Investment v Kyrgyz Mobil Tel [2011] UKPC 7 at [95]).
- Comity requires cogent evidence, but there is no rule against examining the question. Otherwise, the worse the system, the less the court could say ([101]).
- In that case, if there was no trial in the Isle of Man there would be no trial anywhere ([151]).
- The Human Rights Act may also require the court to address fair-trial concerns.
- **Overall.** The question is whether the interests of all the parties and the ends of justice require a stay. The limbs are "pointers rather than boundary marks". Appeals against the first-instance evaluation should be rare.
