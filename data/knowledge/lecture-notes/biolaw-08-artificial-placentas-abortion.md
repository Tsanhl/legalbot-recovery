## Legal framework: England and Wales
- Offences Against the Person Act 1861, s 58: unlawfully procuring a miscarriage with intent (maximum life). Section 59 covers supplying the means.
- Infant Life (Preservation) Act 1929, s 1: child destruction, meaning intentionally causing the death before birth of a child "capable of being born alive". A fetus of 28 weeks or more is presumed capable; in practice 24 weeks is now used.
- **Abortion Act 1967, s 1.**

## How artificial placentas might change things
- **Child destruction.** The test is whether the fetus is "capable of being born alive", and that is movable. Parliament moved the threshold from 28 to 24 weeks in 1990, and C v S [1988] considered evidence on an 18-week fetus.
- Roe v Wade and Casey protected choice before viability. Dobbs v Jackson (2022) removed the federal right and left the matter to the states.
- **Early abortion stays lawful.** Without amending the Abortion Act, early abortion remains lawful under s 1(1)(a) whatever the technology.
- The Act requires no particular method. Method is a matter of patient choice among clinically reasonable options (Montgomery v Lanarkshire HB [2015] UKSC 11).

## What is "miscarriage"? Is fetal extraction for ectogestation a crime?
- **The gap.** The term is undefined in the 1861 and 1967 Acts. Munby J in R (Smeaton) v Secretary of State for Health [2002] EWHC 610 (Admin) defined it as "the termination of an established pregnancy".
- Premature delivery did not exist in 1861, and the Act refers to violent means and noxious things (Alghrani and Brazier 2011).
- The always-speaking statute principle (R v Ireland; Smeaton) cuts both ways.
- Before 24 weeks, extraction is lawful under s 1(1)(a) only if the risk of continuing exceeds the risk of transfer, which may be hard to show.

## Moral arguments
- **Thomson's violinist (1971).** No one is obliged to make large sacrifices to keep another alive.
- **The detachment argument.** Some say the right is to detach, not to cause death, so transfer should be required if it is no worse than abortion (Hendricks 2018; Stratman 2021). Blackshaw and Rodger (2019) would require burdens "significantly but not substantially worse" than abortion.
- Horn (2020): even if transfer were as easy as a pill, forcing it as an alternative to abortion is unacceptable.
- Few seek abortion only to avoid pregnancy's physical burdens (Jackson 2008).
- Control over whether one reproduces is central to identity and dignity (Robertson 1994).
- **The adoption analogy** (Singer and Wells 1984) compares a transferable fetus to a newborn given up for adoption. Critics say it is a flawed comparison: the entity does not yet exist as a person, and social expectations on "mothers" differ.
