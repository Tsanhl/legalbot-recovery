## Current lie detection
- **UK uses.** Polygraph testing is used in offender management: under the Domestic Abuse Act 2021 (a pilot for released domestic abuse offenders) and the Counter-Terrorism and Sentencing Act 2021 (for terrorist offenders on licence). It is not admissible as trial evidence.

## Neuroimaging
- testamentary capacity (Carr v Thomas [2008] EWHC 2859 (Ch));
- unfitness to plead (R v Sharif [2010] EWCA Crim 1709);
- personality change after brain injury (Meah v McCreamer [1985] 1 All ER 367);

## Emerging mind reading
- **Reconstruction.** fMRI decoding can reconstruct viewed images and film (Nishimoto and Gallant, 2011), imagined images, and, with AI, continuous language. Tang et al (Nature Neuroscience, 2023) decoded the gist of heard or imagined speech.

## In the courtroom
- **United States v Semrau (2010, federal).**
- **Wilson v Corestaff (NY, 2010).** fMRI evidence of a witness's truthfulness was inadmissible under Frye, because credibility is for the jury.
- State of Maharashtra v Sharma (2008): Brain Electrical Oscillation Signature (BEOS) profiling showing "experiential knowledge" of the poisoning was admitted as supplementary evidence, and the defendant was convicted.
- State of Maharashtra v Bhoi: BEOS evidence was accepted as corroborative under s 45 of the Indian Evidence Act.
- Later, Selvi v State of Karnataka (2010) held that compelled narco-analysis, polygraph and BEOS tests violate the right against self-incrimination.
- Slaughter v State (Okla 2005): P300 evidence was rejected as insufficiently tested.
- Harrington v State (Iowa 2001): P300 evidence was considered, but the conviction was overturned on other grounds, making the evidence moot.

## Rights implications
- Article 8 ECHR is engaged by "surveillance of the inner life". Current imaging cannot yet truly read thoughts, and arguably resembles existing physical indicators of mental state.
- Blunt v Park Lane Hotel [1942] 2 KB 253.
- Strasbourg allows limits: Murray v UK (1996) on adverse inferences, and Saunders v UK (1997) on compelled statements.
- Bublitz (2013): the right to alter one's mental states with neurotools, and to refuse to do so.
- **Mental integrity.** Article 3 of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights protects physical and mental integrity.
