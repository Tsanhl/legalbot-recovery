## Forum non conveniens: comparisons
- **Australia.** A stay is granted only if the local court is a "clearly inappropriate forum" (Voth v Manildra Flour Mills (1990)). The test is more deferential to the claimant's choice, especially where a local consumer-type claimant sues a powerful foreign defendant.

## Service out of the jurisdiction
- **Stage 1: the claimant's without-notice application for permission** (CPR 6.36–6.37). The claimant must show:
- a good arguable case that the claim falls within a gateway in PD 6B para 3.1;
- that England is clearly the appropriate forum (CPR 6.37(3)). This is Spiliada with the burden reversed.
- **No permission needed** (CPR 6.33(2B)):
- where the court has jurisdiction under the Hague Choice of Court Convention 2005 through an exclusive choice of court agreement;

## Effect of jurisdiction agreements
- **First question: does the 2005 Hague Convention apply?** If it does and the agreement is valid, the chosen English court must hear the case and cannot stay in favour of another court.

## Anti-suit injunctions (ASIs)
- For an English jurisdiction clause, no permission is needed (CPR 6.33(2B)).
- For an arbitration seated in England or unseated, permission comes under CPR 62.5(1)(c).
- For a foreign-seated arbitration, permission comes under CPR 6.37 and PD 6B, for example the gateway at para 3.1(6)(c) for a contract governed by English law.
- **Source of the power.** Senior Courts Act 1981, s 37 ("just and convenient"). The power is confirmed by, not derived from, the statute; it goes back to pre-1873 equity (Fourie v Le Roux [2007] UKHL 1).
- **Brussels limits (now gone).** ASIs against EU or Lugano courts were incompatible with the Brussels regime: Turner v Grovit (vexation) and Allianz v West Tankers (arbitration). These limits no longer apply after Brexit.
- **The principle.** An ASI is granted where needed to avoid injustice, meaning conduct "contrary to equity and good conscience". Equity is not to be confined by categories (Castanho v Brown & Root [1981] AC 557).
- The test does not mirror forum non conveniens. A mere disagreement about the natural forum is not enough, since that would breach comity (SNI Aérospatiale v Lee Kui Jak [1987] AC 871).
- Comity requires caution. It is stronger the closer the foreign court's connection to the dispute. An ASI is an indirect interference with foreign justice (British Airways v Laker [1985] AC 58).
- Barclays Bank v Homan [1993] BCLC 680 (Hoffmann J, approved on appeal): intervention is justified where a foreign court would assert exorbitant jurisdiction contrary to international law, or where policies diverge so much that comity yields.
- The Angelic Grace [1995] 1 Lloyd's Rep 87 (CA): Millett LJ said there is no need for diffidence. An injunction should be granted on the "clear and simple ground that the defendant has promised not to bring" the proceedings, because damages are inadequate. The court needs a good reason not to grant it.

## UniCredit Bank GmbH v RusChemAlliance LLC [2024] UKSC 30
- **Facts.**
- Bonds were issued by a German bank to secure German contractors' performance for a Russian gas project. They were governed by English law, with ICC arbitration seated in Paris.
- After EU sanctions, the contractors stopped work and the bank refused payment.
- RusChem sued in the St Petersburg Arbitrazh Court under article 248.1 of the Arbitrazh Procedural Code (2020). That provision gives Russian courts exclusive jurisdiction over sanctions-related disputes and treats foreign arbitration clauses as incapable of performance. The Russian court held the clause inoperative but stayed its proceedings pending the English case.
- **Issues.**
- Serving the ASI out of the jurisdiction required a gateway: was the arbitration agreement governed by English law? Held: yes, following Enka v Chubb.
- Was England the "proper place"?
- At first instance the court held it lacked jurisdiction. The Court of Appeal ([2024] EWCA Civ 64, Males LJ) granted the ASI, since no French remedy existed and there was no unwarranted interference with the seat court.
- **Supreme Court (Lord Leggatt, unanimous).**
- Forum non conveniens is the wrong lens. There need not be only one proper court to stop a breach of an arbitration agreement ([73]). The question is whether to enforce the parties' bargain ([74]).
- Following Airbus v Patel [1999] 1 AC 119 and IPOC v CT-Mobile (Bermuda CA), where an ASI supports a third forum, the test is whether English intervention is consistent with comity, not whether England is the most suitable forum ([77]).
- Comity was not offended. Russia had itself departed from its New York Convention duties, and French law evidence showed France would not object. The English governing law of the arbitration agreement gave a substantial connection ([83]).
- Enka is qualified. The power to grant an ASI is not part of the supervisory or supporting jurisdiction over arbitration ([95]–[96]). The seat courts supervise the arbitration, but they are not the only courts able to prevent a breach of the agreement to arbitrate ([99]–[100], adopting IPOC).
- Neither the French courts nor the arbitrators could give an effective remedy ([101]). Arbitral orders lack coercive force and contempt sanctions, and RusChem had already ignored its contractual promise ([108]).
- **Aftermath.**
- A Russian court ordered UniCredit to procure the lifting of the ASI, on pain of a €250 million penalty.
- The Court of Appeal later discharged the injunctive parts of its order at UniCredit's request, while the jurisdiction rulings stand ([2025] EWCA Civ 99).
- This illustrates the practical limits of ASIs against a hostile forum.
- **Debate points:**
- Is comity the right test?
- Should the forum non conveniens analysis apply?
- How much weight should Russian law's override of the New York Convention (article II(3): agreements "inoperative or incapable of being performed") receive?
- Is pacta sunt servanda alone a sufficient justification?

## Arbitration Act 1996, s 6A (added by the Arbitration Act 2025)
- **The rule.** The law of an arbitration agreement is the law the parties expressly choose for it. Failing that, it is the law of the seat.
- **Main-contract choice.** A choice of law for the main contract is not an express choice for the arbitration agreement.
- **Effect.** This reverses Enka's default for arbitrations subject to the Act. Facts like UniCredit's would now point to French law.
