# Land law: themes and building blocks
- The personal/proprietary divide (for example licence versus lease); the classic tests for proprietary status are that a right be definable, identifiable by third parties, capable of assumption by them, and have some permanence (National Provincial Bank v Ainsworth [1965]).
- Tenure, estates and interests: all land is ultimately held of the Crown (feudal tenure; bona vacantia returns to the Crown). Since the LPA 1925, s 1, only two legal estates exist: the fee simple absolute in possession (freehold) and the term of years absolute (leasehold). Interests (easements, mortgages) are lesser proprietary rights that can bind purchasers.
- Two title systems: registered land (about 88% of titles, LRA 2002) and unregistered land (LPA 1925, Land Charges Act 1972 and common-law rules).
