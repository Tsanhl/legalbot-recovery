# Unregistered land
- Step one is always to ask whether the third-party right is legal (LPA 1925, s 1(2)) or equitable (s 1(3)). Even an unenforceable right may still bind the original parties in contract.
- Commercial interests registrable as land charges (Land Charges Act 1972, s 2), such as estate contracts (C(iv)), restrictive covenants and equitable easements created after 1925 (D). Registration is actual notice to all (LPA 1925, s 198). Non-registration makes the interest void: C(iv) and D against a purchaser of a legal estate for money or money's worth (LCA 1972, s 4(6)); others against a purchaser for value of any interest. Actual notice does not save an unregistered charge (Midland Bank v Green [1981]).
- Family interests (beneficial interests under trusts), governed by overreaching (LPA 1925, ss 2, 27): paying capital money to two trustees or a trust corporation detaches the interest from the land into the proceeds.
- Land charges are registered against names, not titles: a search against the right name that misses a charge registered in the wrong name protects the purchaser (Diligent Finance v Alleyne (1972)); a search in the wrong name does not (and purchasers should search variants: Oak Co-operative BS v Blackburn [1968]).
- Doctrine of notice: a bona fide purchaser of a legal estate for value without notice takes free (Pilcher v Rawlins (1872)). Notice may be actual, imputed (through an agent) or constructive, meaning what reasonable inspection of deeds and land and inquiries would have revealed (LPA 1925, s 199; Hunt v Luck [1902]; Caunce v Caunce [1969]; Kingsnorth Finance v Tizard [1986]). A later purchaser from equity's darling also takes free (Wilkes v Spooner [1911]).
