## Licences
- A licence makes lawful what would otherwise be trespass; it passes no interest in the land (Thomas v Sorrell (1673)).
- Bare licence (gratuitous permission, express or implied), revocable on reasonable notice, after which the licensee becomes a trespasser (Robson v Hallett [1967]). The scope is limited to the permitted use (The Calgarth [1927]). It is personal and does not bind purchasers.
- Contractual licence (for consideration): premature revocation can be restrained by injunction depending on the contract's terms (Hardwicke v Johnson [1978]); it binds only the contracting parties.

## Leases
- A lease is a legal estate, a term of years absolute (LPA 1925, s 1(1)(b)).
- Essential characteristics: exclusive possession, for a term certain (fixed or periodic), at a rent or premium (Street v Mountford [1985] AC 809). Rent may not be strictly essential.
- Labels are not decisive (Aslan v Murphy [1990]; Antoniades v Villiers [1990]).
- Shams and pretences: terms never intended to be acted on, not reflecting reality, or which neither party regards as effective are disregarded (Antoniades); genuine arrangements are respected (Hilton v Plustitle [1989]).
- Retained keys: kept for emergencies or meter reading suggests tenancy; kept for frequent services actually provided suggests licence (Aslan v Murphy).
- Services: whether the provider may enter at will and whether the services are substantial and actually delivered (Street; Appah v Parncliffe Investments [1964]; Markou v Da Silvaesa (1986)).
- Supervisory control over the space makes the occupier a lodger (Westminster CC v Clarke [1992]; Oxford Overseas Student Housing v Mukherjee).
- Family or social arrangements lacking intention to create legal relations are licences (Marcroft Wagons v Smith [1951]; Garland v Johnson).
- Service occupiers whose accommodation goes with their job are licensees (Barnes v Barratt [1970]).
- Exclusive occupation is not necessarily exclusive possession (Radaich v Smith (1959)).
