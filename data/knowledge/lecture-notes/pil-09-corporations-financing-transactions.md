## Winding up
- **Meaning.** Realising assets, paying creditors and returning any surplus to members before dissolution. The liquidator may obtain an order vesting the company's property in them (Insolvency Act 1986, s 145).
- **Law of incorporation.** "The law that creates can destroy" (Lazard Bros v Midland Bank [1933] AC 289).
- Over UK-registered companies (Insolvency Act 1986, s 117).
- Over unregistered, including foreign, companies (s 221(5)) where the company is dissolved or has ceased business, is unable to pay its debts, or where winding up is just and equitable.
- **Core requirements** (Stocznia Gdanska v Latreefers (No 2) [2001] 2 BCLC 116 (CA)):
- **Example.** Re A Company (No 003102 of 1991) [1991] BCLC 539 wound up a Guernsey company with assets in Portugal. Its loans were negotiated and drawn down in England, it did business and took legal advice in England, and creditors were likely to benefit.
- **Cross-border insolvency.** Recognition of foreign proceedings, access for foreign representatives, cooperation and coordination (Cross-Border Insolvency Regulations 2006, implementing the UNCITRAL Model Law; Insolvency Act 1986, s 426).

## Financing transactions
- Re Helbert Wagg [1956] Ch 323: a loan governed by German law was affected by a German decree requiring payment in marks to a Berlin agency.
