## Legal parenthood
- Identity: legal rules act as a sieve that makes some relationships visible and leaves others legally non-existent (Swennen and Croce 2016).
- Motherhood is proved by birth (The Ampthill Peerage [1977] AC 547).
- HFEA 2008, s 33: the woman who carries or carried the child, and no other woman, is the mother.
- Father: the husband or civil partner (s 35), or a man meeting the agreed fatherhood conditions (s 37); at common law, the genetic father.
- Second female parent: the wife or civil partner (s 42), or a woman meeting the agreed conditions (s 44).
- Egg donation and reciprocal IVF in lesbian couples show that gestation is treated as mothering (Mulligan 2024).
- The rule ignores the intentions of those undertaking pregnancy, notably surrogates and intended parents (Mahmoud and Romanis 2023).
- Stakeholders describe the law as archaic and muddled. Surrogacy law reform (Law Commission 2023) deferred the parenthood issue.
- A machine cannot be a legal mother (Alghrani 2018; Hendricks 2017).

## Artificial placentas and sex/gender equality
- De Beauvoir (1949) and Firestone (1970) located women's oppression in childbearing.
- Smajdor (2007) argues that ectogestation is imperative so women can reproduce as men do, without risk to health, wellbeing and bodily integrity.

- Care work after birth drives most inequality (Jackson 2008). Removing pregnancy does not redistribute childcare. The deeper problems are the social devaluing of care and structural barriers to sharing it.
