## Rome II: timing after Brexit
- **The retained version** applies to later events (SI 2019/834, reg 11).
- **Date that counts.** The date of the event giving rise to damage, not the date of the damage itself (Article 31).
- **Possible replacement.** The Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023 power to restate or replace Rome I and II expired on 23 June 2026. Check whether it was used.

## Before Rome II
- **Common law: double actionability** (Phillips v Eyre (1870); Boys v Chaplin [1971] AC 356; Red Sea Insurance v Bouygues [1995] 1 AC 190).
- **Private International Law (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1995, Part III.**
- General rule: the law of the country where the events constituting the tort occur (s 11). Where the elements are split between countries, sub-rules apply (the place of injury for personal injury, of damage for property, and otherwise the most significant element).
- The law of another country may displace it where that law is substantially more appropriate (s 12).
- Defamation is excluded (s 13), so the common law rules continue for it.

## Rome II
- **Scope (Article 2).** Non-contractual obligations arising from tort or delict, unjust enrichment, negotiorum gestio and culpa in contrahendo.
- **Exclusions (Article 1(2)):**
- **General rule (Article 4).**
- If the defendant and victim are habitually resident in the same country when the damage occurs, that country's law applies (Article 4(2)).
- Otherwise, the law of the country where the damage occurs (Article 4(1)), irrespective of where the event occurred or where indirect consequences are felt. Damage "occurring" is distinct from damage "felt", and locating it can be hard.
- Escape clause: another country applies if the tort is manifestly more closely connected with it, for example through a pre-existing contract (Article 4(3)).
- Article 4(1) usually points to the host state where a subsidiary or supplier caused harm, which may have weak law or governance.
- Suggested reforms: a ubiquity option like Article 7, which lets environmental-damage claimants choose the law of the event; or treating due diligence duties (the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive) as overriding mandatory rules under Article 16.
- **Party choice (Article 14).**
- A domestic situation's non-derogable rules still apply (Article 14(2)–(3)).
- Special rules cover product liability (Article 5), unfair competition (Article 6), environmental damage (Article 7), IP (Article 8, where the lex loci protectionis applies and cannot be displaced by choice) and industrial action (Article 9).
- No renvoi (Article 24).
- **Scope of the applicable law (Articles 15 and 22):**
- How loss is proved, such as what expert evidence is needed, remains procedural for the forum (Wall v Mutuelle de Poitiers [2014] EWCA Civ 138).

## Property
- **Movables and immovables.** English law uses this division for conflicts purposes, not realty and personalty (Re Hoyles [1911] 1 Ch 179). The lex situs classifies the property. The lex fori decides where property or rights are located (Rossano v Manufacturers Life [1963] 2 QB 352).
- Title is governed by the lex situs at the time of the event said to affect it (Cammell v Sewell (1860); Winkworth v Christie Manson & Woods [1980] Ch 496, where Italian law applied to stolen art sold in Italy).
- UK bearer share warrants were abolished by the Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015, s 84.
- Situated where the debtor resides, because that is where it can be enforced (Dicey, Morris and Collins at 23-026; New York Life v Public Trustee [1924] 2 Ch 101; Alloway v Phillips [1980] 1 WLR 888).
- Kwok Chi Leung Karl v Commissioner of Estate Duty [1988] 1 WLR 1035 (PC): a non-negotiable promissory note payable in Liberia by a Liberian company was situated in Liberia, the place of performance where the company was resident. It was therefore outside Hong Kong's estate duty.
- **Assignment** (Rome I, Article 14).
- The UN Convention on the Assignment of Receivables in International Trade 2001 (not in force) applies the law of the assignor's location to priority (Article 30), subject to insolvency preferences in the forum.
- Macmillan v Bishopsgate (No 3) [1996] 1 WLR 387 (CA): Maxwell's nominee pledged shares in Berlitz, a New York company, to London banks in breach of a nominee agreement.
- What a share is: the certificate is only prima facie evidence of title (Companies Act 2006, s 768; no notice of trusts on the register, s 126). The share is a bundle of statutory and contractual rights (IRC v Crossman [1937] AC 26).
- The Hague Securities Convention is in force from 2017 for Mauritius, Switzerland and the US only.
- MCC Proceeds v Bishopsgate (No 4) [1999] CLC 417 applied New York law without choice-of-law analysis.
- **IP.** The lex loci protectionis governs title, assignability and infringement (Rome II, Article 8).
- Shares follow the law of incorporation. Williams & Humbert v W&H Trade Marks (Jersey) [1986] AC 368: Spanish expropriation of Rumasa's shares was effective, because title had already passed in Spain and there was nothing left to enforce.
