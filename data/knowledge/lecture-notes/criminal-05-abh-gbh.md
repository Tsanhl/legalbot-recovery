## Assault occasioning ABH (OAPA 1861, s 47): either way, maximum five years
- Actus reus: an assault or battery that causes (occasions) actual bodily harm; ordinary causation rules apply (Savage and Parmenter [1992]; Roberts (1972)).
- ABH is any hurt or injury likely to interfere with health or comfort that is more than transient or trifling (Donovan [1934]). It includes loss of consciousness (T v DPP [2003]) and cutting hair (DPP v Smith [2006]), and recognised psychiatric illness (not mere fear or panic), which needs expert evidence (Chan-Fook [1994]; Ireland and Burstow [1998]; D [2006] EWCA Crim 1139).
- CPS charging standards: grazes, scratches, minor bruising, swelling and reddening are usually battery; damaged teeth or bones, extensive bruising, cuts needing stitches and loss of consciousness are ABH. The victim's age and health are relevant (Bollom [2003] EWCA Crim 2846).
- Mens rea: only that of the assault or battery (a constructive offence).

## Wounding or inflicting GBH (s 20): either way, maximum five years
- Wound: a break in the whole skin (dermis and epidermis: Moriarty v Brookes (1834)), including the inner skin of the cheek or lip (Smith (1837)). Internal bleeding (JCC v Eisenhower [1984]) and a mere scratch (Morris [2005]) are not wounds. A wound need not be GBH (a needle prick) and GBH need not be a wound (a broken collarbone: Wood (1830)).
- GBH means really serious harm, or "serious" harm is an acceptable direction (DPP v Smith [1961]; Saunders [1985]). It need not be permanent or life-threatening (Ashman (1858)), may be the totality of injuries (Birmingham [2002]), takes account of the particular victim (Bollom; Golding [2014] EWCA Crim 889), and includes serious psychiatric illness.
- "Inflict" is now effectively the same as "cause": no direct force is needed (Ireland and Burstow: phone calls; Dica [2004]: HIV transmission; Brady [2006]).
- Mens rea: "maliciously" means intention or recklessness as to some physical harm, however minor; foresight of serious harm is not needed (Mowatt [1968]; Savage and Parmenter).

## Wounding or causing GBH with intent (s 18): indictment only, maximum life
- Actus reus: wounding or causing GBH.
- Mens rea: intention (direct or oblique) to cause GBH, or intention to resist or prevent lawful arrest or detention combined with malice (foresight of some harm) as to the injury. An intent merely to wound is not enough (Taylor [2009] EWCA Crim 544).
- Illustration: stabbing someone in the abdomen, requiring surgery to a punctured liver, points to s 18 if intent to cause serious harm can be inferred, otherwise s 20.
