## The CPR culture change (England and Wales)
- **Litigation as a last resort.** Under the CPR 1998, costs orders reflect conduct before and during proceedings. A party who wins on the law can still lose its costs, or pay the other side's, for unreasonable conduct.
- **Active case management** includes encouraging ADR under the overriding objective.
- **Costs rules.**
- England: costs follow the event. US: each side bears its own.
- Standard basis: doubt is resolved for the paying party, and proportionality applies.
- Indemnity basis: doubt is resolved for the receiving party, and proportionality is disregarded.
- An unreasonable failure to mediate can justify indemnity costs.

## Case law development
- **Early encouragement:** Cowl v Plymouth CC [2001] EWCA Civ 1935 and Dyson v Leeds CC.
- **Dunnett v Railtrack [2002] EWCA Civ 303.** The successful party was denied its costs for refusing ADR that the court had suggested. This was the first move from encouragement to quasi-compulsion.
- **Others:** Hurst v Leeming, SITA v Wyatt, Valentine v Allen and Leicester Circuits v Coates.
- **Halsey v Milton Keynes General NHS Trust [2004] EWCA Civ 576.**
- Applied in Reed Executive v Reed Business Information, Burchell v Bullard [2005] EWCA Civ 358 (which warned lawyers to advise on ADR), Re Midland Linen and Chaudhry v Yap.
- Unjustified delay can attract sanctions (Costain v Haswell).
- Ignoring an invitation to mediate is itself unreasonable (PGF II SA v OMFS Co [2013] EWCA Civ 1288).
- Others: Thakkar v Patel, Garritt-Critchley v Ronnan, Nigel Witham v Smith, Wethered Estate v Davis, Northrop Grumman v BAE, Beattie v Canham.
- **Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil CBC [2023] EWCA Civ 1416.**
- It relied in part on the Civil Justice Council report on compulsory ADR (2021), which concluded that compulsion is compatible with Article 6 because parties need not settle and can return to court.
- The CPR were then amended to require courts to consider ADR as part of the overriding objective (in force October 2024). Verify the current text of the CPR.

## Consent and compulsion
- The US ADR Act 1998 lets federal district courts compel mediation, and states have schemes.
- Belgium, Germany, Greece and Italy have mandatory schemes without successful Article 6 challenges (Alassini).
- EU Mediation Directive, Article 5(2): compulsory mediation is permitted if access to justice is preserved.
- Mediation must be a condition precedent, not the only route to a decision. In that setting, costs sanctions do not breach Article 6.
