## Nature
- **How it works.** Duress does not negate the actus reus or mens rea. It is an excuse, a "concession to human frailty" (Howe [1987] AC 417). If it succeeds, D is acquitted completely.
- Duress of circumstances: no one demands a crime, but the circumstances threaten death or serious injury and the offence is the only way to escape (Willer (1986) 83 Cr App R 225).
- **Burden.** D bears an evidential burden. The prosecution must then disprove duress beyond reasonable doubt (Bone [1968] 1 WLR 983).

## The Graham test (Graham (1982) 74 Cr App R 235; approved in Howe and Hasan [2005] UKHL 22)
- **Subjective limb.** Was D, or may D have been, impelled to act because, as a result of what D reasonably believed the threatener had said or done, D had good cause to fear death or serious injury?
- **Objective limb.** Would a sober person of reasonable firmness sharing D's characteristics have responded in the same way?

## Exclusions
- Duress is not available for murder (Howe), attempted murder (Gotts [1992] 2 AC 412) or, probably, treason.
- It is available for s 18 GBH and for conspiracy to murder (Ness [2011] Crim LR 645).
- Earlier authority required knowing membership of a violent gang (Sharp [1987] QB 853; Fitzpatrick [1977] NI 20). Shepherd (1988) 86 Cr App R 47 accepted that the defence may survive where D had no reason to expect violence.

## Element 1: the threat and the demand
- Death or serious personal injury (Hudson and Taylor [1971] 2 QB 202).
- Not enough: threats to property (M'Growther (1746)), threats of serious psychological harm (Baker and Wilkins [1997] Crim LR 497), or threats to expose D's sexual conduct (Singh [1973]; Valderrama-Vega [1985] Crim LR 220).
- **Mistaken belief.** A reasonably believed threat suffices even if none existed, for example an unloaded gun (Safi [2003] EWCA Crim 1809; Graham).
- **Source of the threat.** It must come from another person, not from D's own condition (Rodger and Rose [1998] 1 Cr App R 143).
- **Who is threatened.** D, or someone for whose safety D would reasonably regard themselves as responsible (Wright [2000] Crim LR 510).
- Contrast Cole [1994] Crim LR 582, where D chose the robbery himself to repay debt collectors and the defence failed, with Ali [1995] Crim LR 303, where the indication was sufficient.

## Element 2: D's response
- Low IQ and mere vulnerability or timidity are excluded (Bowen [1996] 2 Cr App R 157).

## Necessity
- Necessity is no defence to murder (Dudley and Stephens (1884) 14 QBD 273).
- It has been recognised in medical contexts: Re A (Conjoined Twins) [2001] Fam 147 and F v West Berkshire HA [1990] 2 AC 1.
