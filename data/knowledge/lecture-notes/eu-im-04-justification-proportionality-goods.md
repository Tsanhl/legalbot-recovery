## Article 36 TFEU derogations
- **The grounds:**
- public morality;
- public policy;
- public security;
- the health and life of humans, animals or plants;
- national treasures;
- industrial and commercial property.
- **The proviso.** The measure must not be a means of arbitrary discrimination or a disguised restriction on trade (Conegate, 121/85). The UK could not seize imported inflatable dolls on morality grounds while similar goods could lawfully be made in the UK.
- **Examples by ground:**
- public security: Campus Oil (72/83), security of oil supply;
- public health: Rosengren (C-170/04), Bluhme (C-67/97) and Sandoz (174/82), which dealt with scientific uncertainty and the precautionary principle.
- **The orthodox rule.**
- Distinctly applicable (directly discriminatory) measures can be justified only under Article 36.
- Indistinctly applicable measures can also rely on mandatory requirements.
- The Court has departed from this for environmental protection (PreussenElektra, C-379/98).
- **Purely economic aims** are never accepted. They may be accepted where linked to a non-economic interest such as the financial balance of a social security system (Decker, C-120/95).

## Mandatory requirements (Cassis de Dijon)
- **An open list.** Examples include consumer protection (Mars, C-470/93; Cassis) and environmental protection (Essent Belgium, C-204/12).

## Worked examples
- **Humanplasma (C-421/09).**
- **Scotch Whisky (C-333/14).**
- The UK Supreme Court upheld the measure ([2017] UKSC 76), finding tax less targeted at harmful drinkers.
