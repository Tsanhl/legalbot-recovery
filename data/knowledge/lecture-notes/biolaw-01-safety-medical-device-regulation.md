## The MDR: Regulation (EU) 2017/745
- **Background.**
- It replaced the Medical Devices Directive. A regulation is directly applicable, whereas the Directive needed transposition.
- In Great Britain the Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (SI 2002/618) continue to apply. Northern Ireland follows EU rules.
- **Structure** (similar to the AI Act):
- scope;
- economic operators' obligations and CE marking;
- identification and traceability (UDI);
- notified bodies;
- classification and conformity assessment;
- clinical evaluation and investigations;
- post-market surveillance and vigilance;
- cooperation;
- confidentiality and data protection;
- final provisions.
- **Scope (Article 1(1)).** Placing on the market, making available and putting into service of medical devices for human use and their accessories, plus clinical investigations in the EU.
- **Definition of a medical device (Article 2(1)).**
- Any instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, implant, reagent, material or other article intended by the manufacturer for a specified medical purpose. The purposes are:
- diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease;
- the same for injury or disability;
- investigation or modification of anatomy or physiology;
- in vitro examination of specimens.
- The device must not achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means.
- Also included: devices for controlling or supporting conception, and products for cleaning or sterilising devices.
- **Software.** Software can itself be a device. SNITEM (C-329/16) held that software with a medical purpose relating to individual patients is a device even if it does not act directly on the body.
- **Accessory (Article 2(2)).** Not itself a device, but intended specifically to enable a device's intended use or to assist its medical function. Standard and custom software raise certification questions (recital 19), for example components of surgical robots.
- **Manufacturer.** The person who makes or fully refurbishes a device, or has it made, and markets it under its own name or trade mark.
- **Adverse event.** Any untoward medical occurrence, disease, injury or clinical sign in the context of a clinical investigation.

## Intended purpose as the gateway
- **The rule.** Device status depends on the manufacturer's intended purpose, shown by labelling, instructions and promotional material (Articles 2(12) and 5).

## Manufacturer obligations (Article 10)
- **Risk management** (Article 10(2)) and a **quality management system** (Article 10(9)), kept up to date and proportionate to the risk class. Series production must stay compliant, and changes in design or standards must be taken into account promptly.
- **Sole responsibility.** On non-cooperation or incomplete information, the competent authority may prohibit, restrict, withdraw or recall the device (Article 10(14)).
- **Financial cover** for product liability under the Product Liability Directive 85/374 (Article 10(16)). The Directive is being replaced by the new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853, which covers software.
- **Annex I general safety and performance requirements.**
- Devices must achieve their intended performance, be safe and effective, and carry acceptable risks when weighed against benefits, according to the state of the art.
- Risk control follows a hierarchy:
- eliminate or reduce risk through safe design;
- take protective measures, including alarms;
- provide safety information and training.
- Users must be told of residual risks.
- Use error must be minimised through ergonomics and design suited to the intended users, whether lay, professional or disabled.

## Enforcement architecture
- **Authorities** have document powers (Article 10(14)), market surveillance powers (Article 93), evaluation of suspected unacceptable risk (Article 94), measures against unacceptable risk (Article 95) and powers over other non-compliance (Article 97).
- **Notified bodies** (Chapter IV) may be public or private bodies designated under Articles 36, 39 and 42. Their independence and resources are debated.
- **Overlap with the AI Act.** AI medical devices are high-risk AI under the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) through its Annex I link to the MDR, so both regimes apply.
