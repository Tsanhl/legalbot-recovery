# Can trustees take climate factors into account when investing?
- Pension trusts are constrained by the scheme's purpose and the Pensions Act 2004, s 255 (activities limited to retirement-benefit activities).
- Cowan v Scargill [1985]: for trusts providing financial benefits the paramount duty is the greatest financial return; a heavy burden lies on anyone arguing that accepting less benefits the beneficiaries.
- Harries v Church Commissioners [1992] (charities): moral objections may be accommodated if there is no risk of significant financial detriment.
- Law Commission (2014, 2018): pension trustees may act on non-financial factors if they have good reason to think members share the concern and there is no risk of significant financial detriment.
- R (Palestine Solidarity Campaign) v Secretary of State [2020] UKSC 16, Lord Carnwath: acceptable where it would not involve a significant risk of financial detriment and members would support it. This was obiter, in a 3:2 decision about local-government scheme directions, and it phrases the test differently from the Law Commission ("significant risk of financial detriment" versus "risk of significant financial detriment"). Practitioner bodies have disputed the Law Commission view.
- Further cases: McGaughey v Universities Superannuation Scheme [2023] EWCA Civ 873 (fossil-fuel investment challenge); Butler-Sloss v Charity Commission [2022] EWHC 974 (Ch).
