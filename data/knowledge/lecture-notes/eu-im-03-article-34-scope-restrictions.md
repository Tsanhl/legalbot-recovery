## Scope and direct effect
- **Cross-border element.** A cross-border element is needed, but a potential effect on trade is enough (Pistre, C-321/94). Effects that are too uncertain and indirect are excluded (Krantz).
- **Vertical direct effect** (Iannelli, 74/76).
- **No horizontal direct effect.** Private conduct falls under competition law (Van de Haar, 177/82).
- **Bodies connected to the state.** Article 34 does apply to bodies controlled by the state (Commission v Ireland (Buy Irish), 249/81).
- **Private bodies with de facto regulatory power.** Article 34 applied to a private certification body in Fra.bo (C-171/11).
- **Duty to act against private obstacles.** The state must act against private obstacles (Spanish Strawberries, C-265/95), but it has a margin of discretion in balancing interests such as protest rights (Schmidberger, C-112/00).

## Defining an MEE
- **Dassonville (8/74).** An MEE is any trading rule capable of hindering, directly or indirectly, actually or potentially, intra-EU trade.
- **Cassis de Dijon (120/78): mutual recognition.**

## The road to Keck
- **Over-reach.** Traders used Article 34 against almost any rule, as in the UK Sunday trading litigation (Torfaen, C-145/88).
- **The underlying question** (AG Tesauro in Hünermund, C-292/92): does Article 34 liberalise trade generally, or does it target only obstacles to trade between Member States?

## Keck (C-267/91 and C-268/91): "certain selling arrangements"
- **The rule.** Selling arrangements fall outside Article 34, and need no justification, if two provisos are met:
- they apply to all relevant traders operating in the national territory; and
- they affect domestic and imported products in the same manner, in law and in fact.
- **If a proviso fails,** the measure is an MEE and must be justified.
- **What selling arrangements are:** rules on when, where, how and by whom goods are sold, and in principle rules on advertising and prices.
- **What they are not:** product requirements, which are always MEEs. Familiapress (C-368/95) involved magazine prize puzzles, which altered the content of the product itself.
- **The provisos in practice:**
- Punto Casa (C-69/93): Sunday closing was a selling arrangement.
- De Agostini (C-34/95): an advertising ban may bear more heavily on imports.
- DocMorris (C-322/01): a ban on internet sales of medicines hit foreign pharmacies harder.
- Ker-Optika (C-108/09): restricting contact lens sales to specialist shops was an MEE.
- **Assessment.** Keck is less of a safe haven than it looks.

## Restrictions on use
- **Cases.** Commission v Italy (Trailers), C-110/05, and Mickelsson and Roos, C-142/05 (jet-skis).

## Resulting taxonomy
- **Critique.** Classification drives the outcome. AG Jacobs in Leclerc-Siplec (C-412/93) proposed a single test: does the measure substantially restrict access to the market?
