## Who is a worker
- **An EU concept, interpreted broadly** (Hoekstra, 75/63).
- **The three essentials** (Lawrie-Blum, 66/85): for a period of time, a person performs services for and under the direction of another, in return for remuneration.
- Contrast the self-employed, who fall under Articles 49 and 56.
- Levin (53/81);
- Kempf (139/85), even where the worker supplements income with social assistance;
- Raulin (C-357/89), an on-call contract.
- **Part of the normal labour market.** The activity must be capable of being regarded as such (Trojani, C-456/02), assessed overall (Genc, C-14/09).
- **Pay level** is irrelevant: a trainee teacher's low salary sufficed in Lawrie-Blum. Payment in kind counts (Steymann, 196/87).
- **Retained status (Article 7(3) of the Directive):**
- Under Article 45 itself, a woman who stops work because of pregnancy retains status if she returns within a reasonable period (Saint Prix, C-507/12).

## First-time job-seekers
- **Rights.** They are covered by Article 45, but with fewer rights.
- **Time to look for work.** They must have a reasonable period. Six months was acceptable, after which they can stay if they show they are continuing to seek work with a genuine chance of success (Antonissen, C-292/89; Article 14(4)(b)).
- Financial benefits intended to facilitate access to the labour market must be granted equally, subject to a genuine link with the host labour market (Collins, C-138/02; Vatsouras, C-22/08).
- Alimanovic (C-67/14) classed most such benefits as "social assistance" excluded by Article 24(2). This leaves Collins with a marginal role.
- **Social advantages.** Job-seekers cannot claim Article 7(2) social advantages (Lebon, 316/85).

## Other beneficiaries
- Returning nationals (Singh, C-370/90).
- Wholly internal situations are excluded (Morson and Jhanjan, 35 and 36/82).

## Rights of workers
- Permanent residence may come early (Article 17 of the Directive).
- It can be justified only under Article 45(3), and proportionately. A nationality condition for private security staff was not justified (Commission v Spain, C-114/97).
- **Public service exception (Article 45(4)).**
- It is an EU concept applied functionally (Commission v Belgium, 149/79).
- A rule is indirectly discriminatory if it affects mainly migrants, is more easily met by nationals, or risks operating to migrants' particular detriment. There is no need to prove an actual disparate effect (O'Flynn, C-237/94).
- Examples: rules linked to nationality (Ugliola, 15/69), residence requirements, and requiring a benefit to be used in national territory (O'Flynn).
- Language requirements can be justified. Groener (379/87) upheld an Irish-language requirement for teachers as promoting national identity, if applied proportionately (see also Article 3 of Regulation 492/2011).

## Social and tax advantages (Article 7(2) of Regulation 492/2011)
- **Broad meaning.** It covers benefits granted to national workers because of their status as workers or residents, or that encourage mobility, even if unconnected with the employment contract. An interest-free childbirth loan qualified in Reina (65/81).
- **Family members** benefit indirectly (Lebon; Cristini (32/75): a large-family rail discount survived the worker's death).
- **Excluded:** advantages tied to the special link of nationality, such as war-service pensions (Even, 207/78).

## Non-discriminatory obstacles
- Bosman (C-415/93): transfer fees;
- Lehtonen (C-176/96): basketball transfer deadlines, since restricting participation restricts employment.
- Rules inherent in organising the activity are not caught, such as selection for international sport (Deliège, C-51/96).

## Against whom Article 45 applies
- **Vertically.** Against the host state and the home state, for example restrictions on exit or return (Singh).
- **Collective regulation.** Against rules collectively regulating employment, such as sports bodies (Walrave and Koch, 36/74; Bosman).
- **Private employers.** Against private employers too (Angonese, C-281/98: a language diploma obtainable only locally).
- **Collective agreements.** Discriminatory clauses are void (Article 7(4) of Regulation 492/2011).

## The worker's family
- **Derived rights.** Family members (defined as in the Directive, of any nationality) derive residence rights.
- **Education (Article 10 of Regulation 492/2011).** Children of a current or former migrant worker residing in the host state have access to education on equal terms.
- This extends to non-dependent children over 21 (Gaal, C-7/94) and to rehabilitative courses (Michel S, 76/72).
