## Case law timeline
- **Daily Mail (81/87).**
- Article 49 gave no right to do so. Companies are creatures of national law, and seat-transfer rules were left to future legislation.
- **Centros (C-212/97).**
- Refusing to register the branch breached Article 49. Choosing the most lenient company law is not in itself an abuse.
- Afterwards, UK incorporations from other Member States rose sharply, driven by set-up costs and minimum capital differences (Becht, Mayer and Wagner (2008) 14 J Corp Fin 241).
- **Überseering (C-208/00).**
- **Inspire Art (C-167/01).**
- **Cartesio (C-210/06).**
- **VALE (C-378/10).**
- Cartesio's phrase "to the extent permitted" does not remove host conversion rules from the scope of Article 49.
- **Polbud (C-106/16).**
- This led on to the Mobility Directive (EU) 2019/2121.

## Social dumping
- **Viking (C-438/05).**
- Article 49 has horizontal effect against trade unions, and the collective action was a restriction.
- **Laval (C-341/05).**
- Under Article 56 and the Posted Workers Directive 96/71, the action was an unjustified restriction beyond the Directive's floor of protection.
- Later developments: Directive (EU) 2018/957 and the Monti II debate.
