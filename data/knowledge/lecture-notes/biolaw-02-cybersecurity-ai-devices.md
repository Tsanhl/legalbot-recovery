## AI Act, Article 15 (high-risk AI)
- **Lifecycle performance.** High-risk systems must achieve appropriate accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity, and perform consistently throughout their lifecycle.
- **Benchmarks and disclosure.** The Commission encourages benchmarks and measurement methods. Accuracy levels and metrics must be declared in the instructions for use.
- **Resilience** to errors, faults and inconsistencies, including through redundancy and fail-safes.
- **Continuous learning.** Systems that keep learning must mitigate biased feedback loops.
- **Resilience to attackers.** Systems must resist attempts by unauthorised third parties to exploit vulnerabilities. Measures should address:
- data poisoning;
- model poisoning;
- adversarial examples or evasion;
- confidentiality attacks;
- model flaws.
- **Other duties.** Authorities, notified bodies and providers of general-purpose AI must also consider security.
- **Critique.** Low-risk AI receives lighter treatment even though it can be a gateway into other systems. The final text is still stronger than the proposal.

## Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)
- **Scope.** Very broad: products with digital elements, including AI not covered by the MDR. Medical devices themselves stay under the MDR. It applies cumulatively with NIS2.
- **Article 1:**
- rules for making such products available;
- essential cybersecurity requirements for design, development and production;
- vulnerability-handling requirements for the expected use period;
- market surveillance and enforcement.
- **Article 13(2).** Manufacturers must assess cybersecurity risks and reflect the results through planning, design, development, production, delivery and maintenance, so as to minimise risk, prevent incidents and limit their impact, including on health and safety.
- **Architecture.** Notified bodies and authorities, with a strong emphasis on testing. Much depends on manufacturers. It is strong in substance but relatively weak for a regulation.

## NIS2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555
- **What it is.** The successor to NIS1, covering the cybersecurity of critical and important entities. Health providers such as hospitals are covered, which reaches AI used in treatment, even outside hospitals.
- **Implementation.** Member States implement it. Compliance with other regimes can ease certification.
- **Article 21.** Robust risk-management measures, with specifics set nationally.
- **Supervision (Chapter VII)** is potentially very strong but untested and complex. Article 33(2) powers over important entities include:
- on-site inspections;
- off-site supervision after the event;
- independent targeted audits;
- security scans;
- requests for information, data and evidence of policies and audits.
- **Penalties (Article 39)** must be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. Member States had to notify their rules by 17 January 2025.
