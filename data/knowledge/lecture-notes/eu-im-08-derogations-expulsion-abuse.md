## General rule (Chapter VI, Articles 27–33)
- **Grounds.** Entry and residence may be restricted on grounds of public policy, public security or public health. The rules codify earlier case law.
- **No economic ends.** High unemployment, for example, cannot be a ground (Article 27(1)). Economic self-sufficiency is instead policed through Articles 7 and 14.

## Public policy and public security (Article 27(2))
- **Personal conduct only.**
- Measures must be based exclusively on the individual's personal conduct.
- General deterrence is not a valid reason (Bonsignore, 67/74).
- **The threat.**
- The conduct must pose a genuine, present and sufficiently serious threat affecting one of the fundamental interests of society.
- A merely presumed or past threat does not qualify.
- A state cannot treat conduct it tolerates among its own nationals as a serious threat (Adoui and Cornuaille, 115/81).
- **Previous convictions** are relevant only as evidence of a current threat.
- **Proportionality** is required.
- **Illustration (two sisters with identical robbery sentences).**
- Sister A: a first offence, good conduct in prison and now employed. There is probably no present threat.
- Sister B: a fourth, escalating offence and caught planning another robbery. A present threat is likely.
- **Factors before expulsion (Article 28(1)):**
- length of residence;
- age and health;
- family and economic situation;
- social and cultural integration;
- links with the country of origin.

## Graduated protection (Article 28)
- **Permanent residents:** expulsion only on serious grounds of public policy or public security.
- **Ten years' residence, or minors:** only on imperative grounds of public security.
- PI (C-348/09) held that serious sexual exploitation of children can amount to imperative grounds, reading public security broadly and not limiting it to threats to the survival of the state.

## Procedural limits
- **No automatic expulsion** following a criminal sentence (Article 33; Calfa, C-348/96).
- **No lifetime exclusion.** A person may seek review after a reasonable period, and in any case after three years (Article 32).
- **Procedural safeguards** apply under Articles 30–31.

## Public health (Article 29)
- **Diseases that qualify:**
- diseases with epidemic potential as defined by the WHO;
- other infectious or contagious parasitic diseases, if the host state also applies protective measures to its own nationals.
- **Timing.** Diseases arising more than three months after arrival cannot justify expulsion.
- **Medical examinations** may be required within three months of arrival only where there are serious indications. They cannot be routine.
- **COVID-19.** Nordic Info (C-128/22) held that general bans on non-essential travel to high-risk zones and entry testing and quarantine are permissible if they comply with Articles 30–32, the Charter, non-discrimination and proportionality.

## Abuse of rights and fraud (Article 35)
- **General power.** States may refuse, terminate or withdraw rights in cases of abuse or fraud, proportionately and with procedural safeguards.
- **Fraud:** deliberate deception to obtain a right, such as forged documents or false material facts.
- **Abuse:** artificial conduct entered into solely to obtain free movement rights, formally meeting the conditions but defeating their purpose. The typical case is a marriage of convenience (recital 28).
