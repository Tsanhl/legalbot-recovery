## Structure
- At law: always a joint tenancy (LPA 1925, s 1(6)), no severance at law (s 36(2)), at most four legal owners (s 34(2); Trustee Act 1925, s 34).
- Deciding which in equity: an express declaration is conclusive (Goodman v Gallant [1986]); then words of severance; then presumptions. Equity follows the law (joint tenancy) unless it prefers a tenancy in common: unequal contributions, loans by two or more lenders, business partners, joint business tenants.

## Severance (equity only; converts to a tenancy in common in proportion to the number of co-owners; must occur before death; cannot be reversed; burden on the party alleging it)
- Written notice (LPA 1925, s 36(2)): an unambiguous immediate intention (Re Draper's Conveyance [1969]; Harris v Goddard [1983]), served on all the other joint tenants; service by delivery to the last-known address counts even if the notice is never read (s 196; Re 88 Berkeley Road [1971]; Kinch v Bullard [1999]).
- Acting on one's own share (Williams v Hensman (1861)): transfer, mortgage, specifically enforceable contract, even a fraudulent mortgage of one's share.
- Mutual agreement of all joint tenants about ownership, not merely proceeds (Nielson-Jones v Fedden [1975]; contrast Hunter v Babbage [1994]); an informal agreement suffices even if later revoked (Burgess v Rawnsley [1975]).
- Mutual course of dealing showing all treat their shares as separate (mutual wills: Re Heys [1914]; physical division: Greenfield v Greenfield (1979)).
- Forfeiture: unlawful killing of one joint tenant by another severs (Forfeiture Act 1982, s 1); the court may modify the rule where justice requires (s 2; Re K [1985]).

## Termination and TOLATA 1996
- Co-ownership ends by union in one owner, sale to a purchaser taking free of the trust, or partition (s 7).
- TOLATA replaced the trust for sale with the trust of land (ss 1, 3, 4). Trustees have the powers of an absolute owner (s 6), subject to limits (ss 6, 8, 11).
- Beneficiaries have a right to be consulted (s 11) and a right to occupy if the trust purposes include occupation and the land is available and suitable (s 12; Chan Pui Chun v Leung Kam Ho [2003]). Trustees may restrict or exclude, but not all beneficiaries, not unreasonably, and not those already in occupation, and they may impose reasonable conditions such as compensation (s 13; Rodway v Landy [2001]).
- Disputes are decided on applications under s 14:
- Between co-owners and with creditors, the court considers the s 15 factors: the settlors' intentions, the trust's purposes, the welfare of minors in occupation, and secured creditors' interests. Mortgage Corp v Shaire [2001] said s 15 changed the old creditor-friendly law, but later cases usually order sale for creditors (Bank of Ireland v Bell [2001]; C Putnam & Sons v Taylor [2009]; Edwards v Edwards [2010]; Fred Perry v Genis [2014]; contrast Edwards v Lloyds TSB [2004]).
- Where a co-owner is bankrupt, the Insolvency Act 1986, s 335A applies instead: after a year the creditors' interests prevail unless circumstances are exceptional (Re Citro [1991]; Re Bremner [1999]; compatibility with human rights: Barca v Mears [2004]; Nicholls v Tan [2006]).
