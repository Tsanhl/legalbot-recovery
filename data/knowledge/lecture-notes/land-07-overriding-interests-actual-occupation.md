# Overriding interests
- Schedule 1 applies on first registration; Schedule 3 applies to later registered dispositions under s 29.
- Para 3: legal easements and profits, but only if within the paragraph's terms (for example known to the purchaser, obvious on reasonably careful inspection, or exercised within the previous year). Equitable easements never override under para 3.

## Actual occupation (Sch 3 para 2)
- Needs (1) a qualifying proprietary interest and (2) actual occupation, and (3) neither exception applies.
- Qualifying interest: must be proprietary (National Provincial Bank v Ainsworth [1965]; Scott v Southern Pacific Mortgages [2014] UKSC 52). Common examples are beneficial interests under a trust, leases, options and estoppel equities. An interest that has been overreached cannot qualify (Flegg).
- Actual occupation is a plain-English question of fact (Williams & Glyn's Bank v Boland). Factors: permanence and continuity of presence, the person's intentions and wishes, the length of and reasons for absence, the nature of the property, and personal circumstances (Link Lending v Bustard [2010]).
- Merely preparatory acts are not enough (Abbey National v Cann [1991]).
- Absence can be consistent with occupation if there is an intention to return and some continuing presence (Chhokar v Chhokar [1984]; Link Lending; Stockholm Finance v Garden Holdings [1995]; AIB v Turner [2016]).
- Context matters: residence for homes; physical presence and control for commercial or empty land (Malory Enterprises v Cheshire Homes [2002]; Pennistone Holdings v Rock Ferry Waterfront Trust [2021]).
- Occupation may be through an agent (Lloyds Bank v Rosset [1991]); a minor's presence may not count (Hypo-Mortgage Services v Robinson [1997]).
- The relevant date is the date of the disposition (Cann).
- The interest overrides only as far as the land actually occupied (reversing Ferrishurst v Wallcite [1999]).
- Exceptions:
- Para 2(c): occupation not obvious on a reasonably careful inspection and the purchaser did not actually know of the interest. Both limbs must be met; the first is objective and concerns visible signs of occupation (Thomas v Clydesdale Bank [2010]).
- Para 2(b): the occupier was actually asked and failed to disclose the interest when they reasonably could have been expected to.

## Alteration and indemnity
- Mistakes are corrected by alteration (Sch 4); an alteration that corrects a mistake and prejudices a proprietor's title is rectification, and indemnity may be payable (Sch 8). The Law Commission recommended technical updates in 2018 (Law Com No 380).
