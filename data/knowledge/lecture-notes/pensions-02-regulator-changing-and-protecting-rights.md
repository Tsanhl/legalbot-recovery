## Pensions Regulator
- **Powers under the Pensions Act 1995:**
- suspend, prohibit, remove and appoint trustees (ss 3–9);
- civil penalties (s 10);
- wind up a scheme (s 11).
- **Objectives (Pensions Act 2004, s 5).** Protect members' benefits, reduce the risk of Pension Protection Fund claims, minimise any adverse impact on the sustainable growth of the employer (DB funding functions), maximise auto-enrolment compliance, and promote good administration.
- **Duty to report breaches (s 70).**
- Privileged "protected items" are excluded (s 311).
- **Codes of practice (ss 90–92).** Breach is not itself actionable, but codes are admissible in evidence and must be taken into account.
- **Pension Schemes Act 2021.** Extended contribution notice powers and created criminal offences (from 1 October 2021).

## Accrued rights: Pensions Act 1995, ss 67–67I
- **Versions.** Different versions apply to modifications made in 1997–2006, 2006–2022, and from 1 August 2022.
- **Subsisting rights (s 67A(6)–(7))** are accrued rights and entitlements. For active members they are measured as if pensionable service ended immediately before the modification, so the future final salary link is not a subsisting right.
- **Prohibited modifications are void.** Converting DB rights into collective money purchase rights (s 67(1A), s 67A(1A)).
- **Protected modifications require consent (s 67A(3)–(3A)):**
- converting DB rights to money purchase;
- converting between individual and collective money purchase;
- reducing a pension in payment.
- **Detrimental modifications require consent or actuarial equivalence (s 67A(4), (10)).** Any other change making benefits less generous.
- **Further requirements.** Trustee approval (s 67E) and a report to members (s 67F). Non-compliance makes the change voidable (ss 67G–67H).
- **Collective money purchase schemes:** Occupational Pension Schemes (Modification of Schemes) Regulations 2006, reg 3(k)–(l).
- **Cases:**
- Aon Trust Corp v KPMG [2005] EWCA Civ 1004: a power to adjust benefits to restore solvency is a power to modify within s 67.
- QinetiQ Trustees v QinetiQ Holdings [2012] EWHC 570 (Ch): the scope of a rule allowing trustees to select a "suitable cost-of-living index".

## Amendment powers and their limits
- Re Courage Group's Pension Schemes [1987] 1 WLR 495: a proviso protecting "benefits already secured" can protect the prospective final salary link.
- Lloyds Bank Pension Trust Corp v Lloyds Bank [1996] PLR 263: member-sanction provisos.
- BBC v BBC Pension Trust [2024] EWCA Civ 767, affirming [2023] EWHC 1965 (Ch): active members' "interests" include past service rights, the final salary link and future accrual, so closing the scheme to accrual engages the proviso.
- **Union-negotiated changes.** South West Trains v Wightman [1998] PLR 113 concerned implementing a union-negotiated limit on pensionable pay.
- **Proper purpose.** British Airways v Airways Pension Scheme Trustee [2018] EWCA Civ 1533: trustees' use of a unilateral amendment power to introduce discretionary increases was held invalid (2–1). A power must not be used for an improper purpose (see also Hillsdown Holdings v Pensions Ombudsman [1997] 1 All ER 862).
- **Classifying employer powers.** Whether an employer power is fiduciary, own-interest, or subject to the implied duty of good faith is a matter of construction (Mettoy Pension Trustees v Evans [1990] 1 WLR 1587; Imperial Tobacco).
- **Implied terms.** Tesco Stores v USDAW [2024] UKSC 28: an implied term prevented dismissal and re-engagement designed to remove "retained pay".

## Future service changes
- **Consultation.** Employers with 50 or more employees must consult for at least 60 days before "listed changes" (Pensions Act 2004, ss 259–261; Occupational and Personal Pension Schemes (Consultation by Employers) Regulations 2006).
- unfair dismissal: Employment Rights Act 1996, ss 94 and 98, including "some other substantial reason";
- dismissal of trustees for acting as trustees is automatically unfair (s 102);
- collective redundancy consultation: Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992, ss 188–194 (20 or more dismissals; 30 or 45 days);
