## What "law" can be chosen?
- **Only the law of a country.** Rome I Articles 3 and 4 refer to the law of a country. Recital 13 allows non-State law or conventions to be incorporated by reference as contract terms.
- Shamil Bank of Bahrain v Beximco [2004] EWCA Civ 19: "subject to the principles of the Glorious Sharia'a" was not a choice of a country's law, so English law, the expressly chosen law, governed.
- Conventions ratified by the forum apply according to their own terms regardless of the parties' agreement. For example, an old reference to the Warsaw Convention 1929 yields to the Montreal Convention 1999 where the forum is party to it.

## Bills of lading and paramount clauses
- Such a clause incorporates the Hague Rules or the enacting statute of a country, sometimes stating that they prevail over conflicting terms (Marifortuna Naviera v Government of Ceylon [1970] 1 Lloyd's Rep 247).

## Scope of the applicable law (Rome I)
- **No renvoi** (Article 20).
- **Article 12(1): matters the applicable law governs:**
- performance. The manner of performance and the steps on defective performance also consider the law of the place of performance (Article 12(2));
- **Capacity** is excluded, apart from the Article 13 protection: a person may invoke their own incapacity only if the other party knew of it or was negligently unaware.
- **Existence and material validity** (Article 10(1)) are governed by the putative applicable law.
- Article 10(2): a party may rely on the law of their habitual residence to show they did not consent, if it would be unreasonable to judge their conduct by the putative law. The classic case is silence treated as acceptance.
- **Egon Oldendorff v Libera Corp [1995] 2 Lloyd's Rep 64 (Mance J).**
- The putative English law governed formation. It was not unreasonable to apply English law under Article 8(2): ignoring the London arbitration clause would defeat ordinary commercial expectations, and the Japanese party must have accepted it or should have.
- The burden is on the party invoking Article 8(2), and the court takes a "dispassionate, internationally minded approach".
- **Formal validity (Article 11).**
- A later change of the applicable law cannot prejudice formal validity (Article 3(2)).

## Displacing the chosen law
- **Purely domestic contracts** (Article 3(3)). Where all the relevant elements are located in one country, a foreign choice cannot override that country's non-derogable rules. Article 3(4), which protected EU law in intra-EU cases, is of limited relevance after Brexit.
- **Protected contracts.** Consumer (Article 6), individual employment (Article 8) and certain immovables contracts.
- **Overriding mandatory provisions (Article 9).**
- Those of the forum always apply (Article 9(2)).
- Example: The Hollandia [1983] 1 AC 565. The Hague-Visby Rules have the "force of law" under the Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1971, so a Dutch law and jurisdiction clause that reduced the carrier's liability below the Rules was void.
- Those of the place of performance: effect may be given where they render performance unlawful (Article 9(3)).
- **Public policy of the forum** (Article 21). Application of the foreign law is refused only if it is "manifestly incompatible" with public policy.

## Foreign illegality at common law
- **Ralli Bros v Compañía Naviera Sota y Aznar [1920] 2 KB 287 (CA).**
- **Foster v Driscoll [1929] 1 KB 470 (CA).**
- This extends the rule to illegality existing at the time of contracting. Commission of prohibited acts within the foreign territory is an essential ingredient (Ispahani v Bank Melli Iran [1998] Lloyd's Rep Bank 133).
- **Regazzoni v KC Sethia [1958] AC 301 (HL).**
- Where the governing law is English, the Ralli rule (a mandatory, rule-triggered result) can operate through English law itself. Where the governing law is foreign, the route is Article 21 public policy.
- Article 9(3) is narrower. It requires an overriding mandatory provision of the place of performance, not any prohibition. It is discretionary ("may"), and it applies only to obligations that have to be or have been performed.
- Would Ralli or Regazzoni be decided the same way today? Consider Article 9(3) and Article 21.
