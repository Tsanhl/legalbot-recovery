## Provisions
- **Article 56 TFEU.** It prohibits restrictions on the provision of services by nationals established in one Member State to recipients in another.
- **Article 57 TFEU.**
- **Article 62 TFEU.** The official authority exception (Article 51) and the Article 52 derogations apply to services too.
- **Secondary law.** Services Directive 2006/123 and the Posted Workers Directive.

## Remuneration
- State education funded from the public purse lacks it (Humbel, 263/86).
- Privately funded education has it (Schwarz, C-76/05).
- **Who pays.** The recipient need not be the one who pays (Bond van Adverteerders, 352/85).
- **No economic link, no service.** In Grogan (C-159/90), student groups distributing abortion information unconnected with the providers were exercising freedom of expression, not providing services.

## Cross-border scenarios
- **The provider moves** to the recipient's state, even if the recipient comes from the provider's own state. Tour guides accompanying their own nationals were protected (Commission v France, C-154/89). Keep the boundary with establishment in mind (Gebhard).
- **The recipient moves** to the provider's state, for example for medical treatment or tourism (Luisi and Carbone, 286/82; Cowan, 186/87).
- **Only the service moves,** for example broadcasting, online services or telephone marketing (Alpine Investments, C-384/93).

## Analysis
- Discrimination, or any measure liable to prohibit, impede or render less attractive the provision of services (Säger, C-76/90).
- Direct discrimination can be justified only under Articles 52 and 62.
