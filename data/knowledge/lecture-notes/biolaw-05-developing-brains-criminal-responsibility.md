## England and Wales position
- **The age.** The minimum age of criminal responsibility (MACR) is 10. Children over 10 face the full criminal law with no defence reflecting their status as children (Elliott (2011) 75 J Crim L 289).
- **The UN view.** A MACR below 12 is not internationally acceptable (CRC General Comment No 10 (2007); General Comment No 24 (2019) recommends at least 14).
- **The Law Commission** (Law Com No 304, 2006): it is unfair to hold criminally responsible someone who cannot choose between right and wrong or cannot act on that knowledge.
- **Smith's jibe** ((1994) 53 CLJ 426): a person is completely irresponsible the day before their tenth birthday and fully responsible once the party is cleared away.

## Concepts of responsibility
- **R v G [2003] UKHL 50.** Ignoring the special position of children in assessing recklessness is unacceptable in a modern civil society.

## History
- Children Act 1908: MACR 7;
- Children and Young Persons Act 1933, s 50: MACR 8;
- Children and Young Persons Act 1963: MACR 10.
- After the Bulger case (1993) it was attacked as contrary to common sense (Home Office, No More Excuses (1997)).
- Section 34 of the Crime and Disorder Act 1998 abolished the presumption. JTB [2009] UKHL 20 held that the defence went too.

## Other jurisdictions
- Roper v Simmons (2005): no death penalty for under-18s. The Court relied on developmental science showing immaturity, vulnerability and poorer self-control.
- Graham v Florida (2010): no life without parole for juvenile non-homicide offenders.
- Miller v Alabama (2012): no mandatory life without parole for juveniles.
