## Concepts
- An internal market adds free movement of goods, persons, services and capital (Article 26 TFEU).
| Goods | Art 34 TFEU (plus Arts 30 and 110) |
| Workers | Art 45 |
| Citizens | Arts 20–21, Directive 2004/38 |
| Establishment | Art 49 |
| Services | Art 56, Services Directive 2006/123 |
| Capital | Art 63 |

## Negative and positive integration
- **Positive integration (harmonisation).** EU legislation, especially under Article 114 TFEU, replaces national rules with common standards.

## The five-step structure of a free movement case
- Effects that are "too uncertain and indirect" are excluded (Krantz, C-69/88).
- Most also have horizontal direct effect. Goods are the exception (but see Fra.bo, C-171/11).
- The state has a duty to act against obstacles created by private parties (Commission v France (Spanish Strawberries), C-265/95; Schmidberger, C-112/00).
- Treaty derogations: Articles 36, 45(3), 52 and 62 TFEU.
- Occasionally, proportionality in the strict sense (balancing): see Commission v Denmark (Danish Bottles), C-302/86.

## Brexit prompt (essay)
- "British jobs for British workers" is direct nationality discrimination contrary to Article 45 TFEU. The public-service exception in Article 45(4) is narrow. The slogan was therefore incompatible with remaining in the internal market.
