# What counts as "land"
- Statutory definition: LPA 1925, s 205(1)(ix) covers physical land, buildings, mines and minerals (corporeal hereditaments) and intangible rights such as easements and rents (incorporeal hereditaments).

## Airspace
- A landowner owns the lower stratum: airspace to the height necessary for the ordinary use and enjoyment of the land and its structures (Bernstein v Skyviews [1978]). Above that (the upper stratum) the owner has no greater rights than the public.
- Intrusions into the lower stratum can be trespass without damage (Kelsen v Imperial Tobacco [1957], an overhanging sign; oversailing cranes, Woolerton & Wilson v Costain [1970]; overhanging branches, Lemmon v Webb (1894)).
- Aircraft in normal flight are protected by the Civil Aviation Act 1982, s 76.

## Finds
- Items embedded in or attached to land: the landowner has the better claim (Elwes v Brigg Gas (1886); Waverley BC v Fletcher [1996]).
- The true owner beats a finder (Moffatt v Kazana [1969]); a finder beats everyone except the true owner, including someone who later takes the item (Armory v Delamirie (1722)).
- Finder versus occupier for items found on land: the finder wins if the item was lost or abandoned and the finder took it into care and control, unless the occupier had shown, before the find, an intention to control the land and things on it (Parker v British Airways Board [1982]). Employees finding in the course of employment find for the employer.

## Treasure (Treasure Act 1996)
- Treasure belongs to the Crown (s 4) and must be reported to the coroner within 14 days of finding (s 8; failure is an offence subject to reasonable excuse).
- Definition (s 1): non-coin objects at least 300 years old with at least 10% precious metal; coins in finds meeting age and metal thresholds; groups of at least 10 coins at least 300 years old; associated objects. A newer significance-based category adds metal objects at least 200 years old that give exceptional insight into national or regional history.
