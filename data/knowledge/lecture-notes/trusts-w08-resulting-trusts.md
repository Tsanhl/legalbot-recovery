# Resulting trusts
- Resulting trusts arise by operation of law (s 53(2) LPA 1925 exempts them from writing). Beneficial ownership "jumps back" to the transferor. Theories of why they arise are contested.
- Automatic resulting trusts arise when an express trust fails or does not exhaust the property (for example surplus funds: Re Gillingham Bus Disaster Fund [1958]; Re West Sussex Constabulary Fund [1971]). They do not arise where the transferor intended to part with the property outright.
- Vandervell saga: shares transferred with an option to repurchase held by the family trust company without saying for whom; the option was held on automatic resulting trust for Vandervell, so he was taxed. A resulting trust "is born and dies without any writing": it ends when the gap in beneficial ownership is filled (Re Vandervell's Trusts (No 2) [1974], Lord Denning).
- Quistclose trusts: money lent for a specific purpose and kept separate is held on trust for that purpose, and if the purpose fails it is held for the lender, so it is protected from the borrower's insolvency (Barclays Bank v Quistclose Investments [1970] AC 567). Claims for a Quistclose trust fail where the payment was not restricted to a purpose (Prickly Bay Waterside v British American Insurance [2022] UKPC 8).
