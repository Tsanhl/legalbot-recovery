## Duties
- Investment: trustees may make any investment an absolute owner could (Trustee Act 2000, s 3(1)), subject to the statutory duty of care (s 1, excludable under Sch 1 para 7), the standard investment criteria (suitability and diversification) and review, and taking proper advice.
- Standard of care: historically the prudent person of business investing for others (Speight v Gaunt; Learoyd v Whiteley). Modern portfolio theory judges risk across the whole portfolio rather than asset by asset (Nestle v National Westminster Bank [1993] 1 WLR 1260; Bartlett v Barclays Bank Trust Co [1980] Ch 515); larger funds may justify more risk (Trustees of the British Museum v A-G). The standard is higher for professionals; exemption clauses are now common.
- Best interests usually means financial interests (Cowan v Scargill [1985] Ch 270), read against the trust's purpose. Ethical and ESG investing is permissible within limits (Harries v Church Commissioners [1992] 1 WLR 1241; Butler-Sloss v Charity Commission [2022] EWHC 974 (Ch)).

## Remedies for breach
- Commercial bare trusts: Target Holdings v Redferns [1996] AC 421 moved to compensating actual loss, judged with hindsight at trial and subject to "but for" causation; AIB Group v Mark Redler [2014] UKSC 58 confirmed this and added that trustees do not insure against market loss and that concealment does not alter liability.
- Co-trustees are jointly and severally liable; equity does not tolerate a sleeping trustee; contribution can be apportioned under the Civil Liability (Contribution) Act 1978.
- Defences and relief: beneficiary consent or acquiescence; impounding a beneficiary's interest (Trustee Act 1925, s 62); exemption clauses, valid except for dishonesty (Armitage v Nurse [1998] Ch 241; Walker v Stones [2001]); court relief for honest and reasonable trustees (s 61; Re Pauling's ST [1964]); limitation of six years (Limitation Act 1980), with exceptions for fraud, property still held by the trustee, future interests and deliberate concealment; laches where the Act does not apply.
